Gen.G completed a 3-0 Swiss Stage run at Worlds 2026 on Sunday by coming from a game down to beat LYON 2-1. The LCK champions are through to the quarter-finals, while LYON must look to Round 4 to join them.

The best-of-three series began at 20:55 on Sunday 25 October 2026.

Earlier in the evening, on the third day of the Swiss Stage, Dplus KIA swept Movistar KOI 2-0. Smash scored the first pentakill of the tournament in game one of that series.

The Swiss Stage runs over five rounds. Teams reaching three match wins advance to the Knockout Stage. Teams reaching three defeats are eliminated. Gen.G’s run began with 1-0 wins over Invictus Gaming and Hanwha Life Esports.

LYON took the opener with two Barons

LYON controlled game one. They took Baron as soon as it spawned at 20 minutes without losing a player. A second Baron followed after a five-versus-five fight, and LYON pushed on to the Nexus. Dot Esports picked out jungler Inspired and bot laner Berserker, on Kalista, as LYON’s key performers. On the other side, Canyon’s Qiyana kept missing his ultimates.

Gen.G responded quickly. A convincing top-lane teamfight at 23 minutes gave them Baron and the Dragon Soul, and the series was level. In the decider, Gen.G lost Galio early in a teamfight. Kiin then joined on Camille via Teleport. Gen.G went on to break the mid inhibitor, collect three dragons and win the final fight.

On paper, the gap between the sides was wide. Gen.G sit fourth in the Elo power rankings with a rating of 1868 and a 118-30 record. LYON are 28th with a rating of 1720 and a 34-28 record.

LYON stay alive while G2 face elimination

The defeat was LYON’s first at the event. They had opened with 1-0 wins over Team Secret Whales and Karmine Corp. They go into Round 4 with a 2-1 record. One more match win would send them to the Knockout Stage.

LYON’s season already includes a significant result against G2 Esports. LYON knocked G2 out of MSI 2026 with a 3-0 win on 10 July 2026, and Inspired was named MVP of the series. In Allen, the LEC’s representative were beaten 1-0 by Top Esports in Round 3.

Bethune sets new single-season NWSL assist record

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Croix Bethune has a record all to herself.

The Kansas City Current midfielder recorded her 11th assist of the regular season Sunday, setting a new NWSL single-season record. Vanessa DiBernardo finished the chance in the 17th minute against the Washington Spirit to put Kansas City ahead 2-1.

Bethune first tied the record in 2024, when she recorded 10 assists in 17 appearances for Washington. She shared the mark with Portland Thorns midfielder Tobin Heath, who recorded 10 assists in 2016, and Utah Royals midfielder Cloé Lacasse, who reached 10 assists this past August.

Selected third overall in the 2024 NWSL Draft out of the University of Georgia, Bethune made an immediate impact in Washington, finishing her debut season with five goals and 10 assists. Her performances earned her both Rookie of the Year and the league’s inaugural Midfielder of the Year award.

Traded to Kansas City in February, Bethune has continued to produce in her first season with the Current. The club entered Sunday’s regular-season finale in fourth place, having already clinched a playoff spot. The new record adds another milestone to a career that has seen Bethune establish herself as one of the NWSL’s standout playmakers.

Zcash has three older shielded pools, and only one of them has a shutdown date. On November 5, NU7 will make the 22,263 ZEC left in Sprout unspendable.

The 346,000 ZEC still in Orchard and the 420,000 ZEC in Sapling face no deadline at all.

But the Zcash specifications include one conditional warning that covers all three pools, and it has been widely misquoted. Let's go through the ZIPs to set out exactly what each document says about each pool, and what it doesn't.

Three Old Pools, One Deadline

Zcash keeps value in separate pools, and each one is tied to a generation of its privacy technology. As of October 25, community tracker ZecStats reports:

  • Sprout, the original pool: 22,263 ZEC, marked "closing"
  • Sapling: 420,000 ZEC, open
  • Orchard: 346,000 ZEC, sealed and migrating
  • Ironwood, the new pool introduced in NU6.3: 4.18 million ZEC

Together, the three older pools hold about 788,000 ZEC. Any full node reports these balances directly through the valuePools field of its getblockchaininfo RPC, so anyone running a node can check them.

The three pools are on very different paths. Sprout has a fixed end. Orchard is closed to new deposits, but no spec sets a closing date. Sapling remains a supported pool.

Sprout: The Only Pool With an End Date

Sprout's end has been planned for years. ZIP 211, written in 2019, stopped new value from entering Sprout. It described that as "a step toward being able to remove the Sprout shielded protocol."

ZIP 2003 takes the final step. It doesn't target Sprout directly. Instead, it bans an entire transaction format: from NU7 onward, "the transaction version number MUST be 5 or 6." Sprout transfers can only be written as version 4 transactions, so Sprout funds can no longer be spent.

ZIP 259, the NU7 deployment spec, sets the activation height at block 3,543,405 on mainnet. It states the result directly: "funds remaining in the Sprout pool become unspendable."

The wording matters. ZIP 2003 says it does not "unissue, burn, or otherwise make Sprout funds permanently unavailable." The coins stay counted in Zcash's issued supply, and the ZIP leaves open "the theoretical possibility of re-enabling v4 transactions, or of adding another facility to retrieve these funds." It also cautions that this "may never be re-enabled."

Recovering Sprout funds was already hard before the deadline. According to ZIP 2003, the retirement of zcashd just before NU6.3 "removed the only maintained software that still provided wallet functionality for Sprout." The pool has barely changed in the last few weeks: the widely reported figure of 22,309 ZEC has fallen only to 22,263, so about 46 ZEC has moved out since.

Orchard: Sealed, Shrinking, but Not Scheduled to Close

Orchard's situation is newer and more unusual. ZIP 258 activated NU6.3 at block 3,428,143 and sealed Orchard after a soundness vulnerability was found and fixed. Since then, the consensus rules have enforced three restrictions:

  • No new value may enter Orchard, including through mining rewards.
  • Transfers between different users' addresses are disabled within Orchard.
  • Value may still leave, including across the "turnstile" into the Ironwood pool.

ZIP 229 explains why: Orchard held "a substantial portion of ZEC" when the vulnerability was fixed, so moving those funds to a fresh pool was meant to restore confidence in Zcash's total supply.

The migration has largely worked. ZecStats counts 3,278,499 ZEC moved out of Orchard, about 90.5% of what was in the pool at NU6.3. That leaves about 346,000 ZEC, roughly 2% of the circulating supply.

What no ZIP includes is a date when Orchard stops working. The rules make Orchard one-way, so holders can leave but can't add or pay anyone else. They don't make it a countdown. ZIP 318, the wallet guide for the migration, focuses on how to move funds without revealing balances. It recommends splitting balances into standard amounts, sending them over time and leaving a small remainder unmigrated. That's guidance on privacy, not a deadline.

Sapling: Still Open, Still Supported

Sapling has the fewest restrictions of the three. Version 6 transactions support it as one of the "supported shielded pools," alongside Orchard and Ironwood, and none of the NU6.3 or NU7 ZIPs restricts it.

There's one catch for Sapling users that many people are missing. ZIP 259 says the version 4 ban covers "all version 4 transactions, including transparent-only and Sapling transactions, not only Sprout transfers." Sapling funds stay spendable after NU7, but only through version 5 or version 6 transactions. Wallets still building version 4 transactions need to switch before the upgrade. Pending or offline-signed version 4 transactions won't confirm afterward, and "re-signing alone cannot make a version 4 transaction valid."

Where the "Inaccessible" Warning Actually Comes From

The sentence behind the "inaccessible funds" headlines appears in ZIP 2005, "Ironwood Quantum Recoverability," and is repeated in ZIP 229:

"Recovery would not be possible for funds still in the Sprout, Sapling, or Orchard pools; all such funds would be inaccessible after their respective protocols are disabled."

The sentence before it explains the context. Zcash's existing shielded protocols depend on discrete logarithms being hard to compute. If an attacker, using a quantum computer or another method, could break that, the protocols might have to be disabled "to prevent such attacks." Only funds in Ironwood would be recoverable, through a recovery protocol designed to stay secure against quantum attacks.

So this is a contingency, not a schedule. It describes what would happen to funds in the older pools if a future cryptographic emergency forced Zcash to shut them down. No ZIP proposes doing that on any date. The practical advice behind it is plain: funds in Sprout, Sapling or Orchard "should be migrated to the Ironwood pool" to be covered by recovery.

What Node Operators and Holders Need Before Block 3,543,405

The software is ready. The Zcash Foundation released Zebra 7.0.0 on October 22. Its changelog says it "supports the NU7 network upgrade on Mainnet, which activates at height 3,543,405." The release moves Zebra's database from format v28 to v29. Zebra 6.x can't read the new format, so the Foundation recommends backing up the old state first. The zcashd compatibility sidecar must also be updated to zebra-compat-v1.3.0. Earlier builds can't follow mainnet past NU7.

This upgrade carries more risk than usual. ZIP 259 waives Zcash's usual rule that old software must shut itself down before an upgrade. The spec warns that "operators of unsupported software may therefore remain online at activation and follow a pre-NU7 consensus branch."

For holders, the guidance from each pool's spec comes down to this:

  • Sprout: move funds before block 3,543,405. Afterward, there's no supported way to spend them.
  • Orchard: no deadline, but the specs consistently point toward Ironwood, the only pool covered by quantum recovery.
  • Sapling: spendable after NU7 through version 5 or version 6 transactions, with no sunset on record.

On November 5, Sprout closes. The 766,000 ZEC in Sapling and Orchard face no deadline, but the specs still recommend moving it to Ironwood.

Lecce vs Juventus: in-form hosts await Spalletti’s stretched side at the Via del Mare

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Juventus face Lecce at the Stadio Via del Mare this evening, and the fixture arrives at an awkward moment for Luciano Spalletti. His side are still looking for a first win since the 3-0 home defeat by Lazio, while Di Francesco’s Lecce come into Matchday 8 on the back of their most convincing result of the season.

Kick-off is at 18:00 Italian time, which is 17:00 in the UK and Ireland.

Lecce arrive in form after a statement win in Udine

Lecce travelled to Udinese last weekend and came away with a 3-0 victory. According to Flashscore, Kialonda Gaspar headed in the opener from a Gallo corner in the 14th minute, and N’Dri doubled the lead in the 25th minute after Tiago Gabriel had gone up for a header. Siebert added the third four minutes from time after a ball was won from Ekkelenkamp.

It was Lecce’s first win in Udine for six years and handed Udinese a fifth straight league defeat. Fatah also hit the crossbar, so the margin could have been even wider. For a side that has spent much of its recent history fighting at the wrong end of the table, it was a timely lift before the visit of Juventus.

Juventus still searching for a response

The picture is very different in Turin. The heavy home loss to Lazio was followed by a 2-2 draw with Rennes in the Europa League, and the injury list has kept growing. Zeki Celik and Nico Gonzalez are the latest names to drop out, as covered in our Celik and Nico Gonzalez injury update, and Spalletti chose not to give a pre-match press conference on Saturday.

The Lazio defeat in Turin left Juventus well short of the Serie A leaders, so Sunday is about more than one result. Three points in Puglia would ease some of the pressure that has built up over the past week.

The last time Juventus beat Lecce

Juventus do not have to look far back for a good memory of this trip. The club’s own retrospective recalls the Matchday 36 meeting at the Via del Mare in May 2026, when Dusan Vlahovic scored after just 12 seconds. Kenan Yildiz started the move, Teun Koopmeiners was involved in the build-up and Andrea Cambiaso delivered the ball for Vlahovic to finish into the roof of the net.

That game stayed lively. Michele Di Gregorio had to make a fine save at the other end, Francisco Conceicao struck the crossbar and Vlahovic had another good chance, all inside the first six minutes. Pierre Kalulu thought he had added a second with around half an hour to play, but VAR ruled it out for a marginal Vlahovic offside in the build-up. The early goal proved enough for the win.

Several of the players involved that day, including Yildiz and Conceicao, are now injured, which shows how much Spalletti’s options have changed since May.

Referee and where to watch

Tuttosport reports that Maresca will referee the match. In Italy it is live on DAZN and Sky. In the United Kingdom the game is shown on DAZN UK and Prime Video UK, in Ireland on DAZN, and in the United States on Paramount+, fuboTV and Peacock, according to the club’s broadcast guide.

DraftKings chief executive Jason Robins says the company’s in-house exchange, DKeX, has become the third-largest prediction market operator in the US just a few months after launch. “We only launched the DraftKings exchange a few months ago, and we’re already the clear No. 3,” Robins said at New York’s NEXTPredict Summit on Thursday, in a Q&A hosted by CNBC’s Contessa Brewer, Yogonet reported.

DKeX went live in late June 2026. According to Aldrin Research figures cited in the report, the exchange was third by notional trading volume for the week to October 18, handling $1.04 billion in trades for a 3.57% share of the market. That was up from a 2.33% share in the week ending September 26. Kalshi remains well ahead in first place, with Polymarket a distant second. Notional volume measures potential contract payouts rather than cash committed, a distinction covered in this look at how prediction-market volume is counted.

A smaller footprint by design

Robins stressed that DraftKings reached that position with fewer markets than its rivals. “We’ve done that with a much more limited state footprint,” he said.

DraftKings Predictions is available in 38 states, but the full range of event contracts is offered in only 18 of them, including California, Florida and Texas. Where DraftKings operates a licensed sportsbook, including Colorado, New York and Pennsylvania, the product is limited to financial event contracts. Robins said the company avoids offering sports contracts in places where it also takes traditional sports wagers. In Nevada, it offers only free-to-play sports contests.

He acknowledged that the exchange would be more prominent if sports contracts were available in New York, but said that route carried too much regulatory risk. “The alternative was we would have had our licenses under threat,” Robins said.

Uncertainty weighs on the stock

The legal status of sports event contracts is still being fought in the courts, with the issue moving toward the US Supreme Court. Robins argued that DraftKings is positioned to benefit whatever the outcome, and that expansion beyond sports, for example into financial contracts, could support the business even if sports contracts are curtailed.

Investors have been less patient. The company’s stock is down more than 40% so far this year, and Robins linked that caution to the unsettled rules around federally regulated exchanges. “Private markets love uncertainty,” he said. “Public markets are the opposite. They do not like uncertainty.”

Responsible gambling questions

Robins also addressed recent criticism of how DraftKings uses artificial intelligence and handles responsible gambling. He said the company does not target problem gamblers and that customers identified as at risk are passed to a dedicated team. “It’s just completely not how we’re built,” he said. The comments come as regulators in several states examine the company’s marketing, including in Louisiana, where DraftKings is operating under a conditional license extension tied to an AI review.

He said little about the Seminole Tribe’s lawsuit against DraftKings in Florida, beyond noting that he found it interesting the company had been singled out when other prediction markets also operate in the state.
