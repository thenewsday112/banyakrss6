Shares of memory chipmaker Micron Technology (NASDAQ:MU) have seen a tough few months. At around $892 as I write, the stock's down about 26% from its late-June record close.

Then, on Oct. 30, China's largest maker of DRAM (dynamic random-access memory, the chips that store data when a phone, PC, or server is running) posted results that are hard to ignore. ChangXin Memory Technologies (SHSE:688825), better known as CXMT, said its third-quarter revenue climbed 613% year over year to around 118.7 billion yuan (about $18 billion). In other words, it sold over seven times as much as it did in the same quarter in 2025.

A rival growing so fast in DRAM deserves attention. But I think CXMT's report says more about memory prices than about Micron's future, and it doesn't change my view that Micron stock is a buy.

Joe Burrow is one four-TD, no-INT game away from elite club

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

The 2026 Bengals have won two, lost two, and then won two. In their last two, quarterback Joe Burrow has been stellar.

In both the 31-24 Week 5 win over the Dolphins and the 35-13 Week 6 win over the Ravens, Burrow threw four touchdown passes and no interceptions. If he does that again on Sunday against the Titans, Burrow will expand the membership of an elite club by 50 percent.

Per NBC Sports research, another four-TD, no-pick game from Burrow would make him only the third quarterback in NFL history to do it for three straight games. Rams quarterback Matthew Stafford accomplished it in 2025. Then-Packers quarterback Aaron Rodgers was the first to ever do it, in 2016.

The Bengals have beaten the Titans four of five times, including a victory over Tennessee in the 2021 postseason. A win would vault the Bengals into second place in the AFC North, in front of the loser of the Browns-Steelers game.

Unless they tie. In that case, the Browns and Steelers would each be 5-2-1, and the Bengals (with a win on Sunday) would be 5-2.
