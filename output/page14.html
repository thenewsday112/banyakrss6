Recap: Miami survives North Carolina 38-35

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →
(Photo by Eston Parker/ISI Photos/ISI Photos via Getty Images) | ISI Photos via Getty Images

Wow. All I can immediately muster as I sit down at my computer.

The Miami Hurricanes remained perfect on Saturday night in Chapel Hill, just barely getting past North Carolina 38-35.

Darian Mensah was 22/29 passing for 274 yards and three scores with no interceptions. Mark Fletcher, Jr. had 13 rushes for 43 yards with two touchdowns and added a 31-yard receiving touchdown. Malachi Toney had seven catches for 75 yards and two touchdowns. Chase Smith, JJ Dunnigan, and Mohamed Toure all led Miami with 9 total tackles each.

As the score indicates, it was a defense optional contest from the start. Miami went 74 yards in 13 plays to take a 7-0 lead. Mensah lobbed a ball towards the sideline in the end zone to Toney, who made an incredible blind catch over the shoulder that I can’t do justice with words for a two-yard score.

UNC punted (I don’t write those words again in this piece, very sadly), on the ensuing drive, but Miami punted it back six plays later.

And that’s when Miami’s 2024 defense trotted out onto the field. The Hurricanes were an absolute disaster as a unit, missing tackle after tackle, particularly on the perimeter, and getting absolutely eaten alive in zone coverage. Carolina went off in the second quarter, putting together an 11-play, 84-yard drive and a 12-play, 75- yard drive, both of which ended in touchdown passes to UNC TE Jelani Thurman, who Miami made look like the greatest college tight end I’ve ever seen.

The Hurricanes had their own touchdown in between, with Mark Fletcher, Jr. lining up wide in the back side of a diamond formation near the sideline. He took a quick pass from Mensah, and the trio in front of him forged a lane that he took 31 yards to the house.

The Hurricanes added a field goal shortly before the halftime whistle for a 17-14 lead.

UNC’s Billy Edwards, who had the game of his life with 341 yards on 28/34 passing with four touchdowns and no picks, rolled right and hit Jordan Shipp in the end zone for a 21-17 lead on the first drive of the second half. Miami answered with their own long drive to go back up 24-21, with Fletcher, bowling in and getting over the goal line, which survived a review.

But I mentioned UNC didn’t punt again, and instead, they kept schooling Corey Hetherman’s defense for the second half. The following drive was a death by 1000 paper cuts, going 16 plays for 75 yards and taking almost eight and a half minutes off the clock. Demon June ran it in from a yard out with under 12 minutes left, and it was Carolina back on top 28-24.

Finally Miami took the lead and didn’t give it back up. Toney finally found himself all alone after a night of being bracketed, with a 31-yard catch and run to energize the drive. Fletcher bowled into the end zone on fourth and 1 at the two, and Miami could breathe again momentarily.

The game turned in a big way when Marquise Lightfoot got home and forced a fumble by Edwards, and Justin Scott fell on it. Six plays later, Toney juked his defender out of his shorts and caught a slant for a 38-28 lead with just over two and a half minutes left.

Miami’s sieve of a defense on the night allowed UNC to go 75 yards IN 55 SECONDS, and Carolina recovered two onside kick attempts, but offsides and illegal block flags — both fully legit — wiped them out. Miami recovered the third attempt and killed the remaining clock.

Survive and advance. On to Notre Dame.

Daylight Saving Time Is Ending, and It Could Mean More Deer on the Road

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Drivers have another reason to be extra careful on the roads after the clocks turn back this weekend: The change to standard time could put more commuters behind the wheel during the hours when deer are most likely to be crossing.

Daylight Saving Time ends at 2 a.m. Sunday, Nov. 1, sending clocks back one hour. The annual change also shifts many evening commutes into the earlier hours of darkness, and that could be a problem for drivers during one of the busiest times of year for deer activity.

A recent report published by Wausau Pilot & Review highlighted research showing that the fall clock change can increase deer-vehicle collisions during evening commuting hours. The research comes from Tom Langen, a biology professor at Clarkson University, and his colleagues, who analyzed more than 86,000 deer-vehicle collisions in New York.

The Time Change Can Put More Cars on the Road at Dusk

Deer-vehicle collisions are already most common around dawn and dusk, when deer are active and visibility is lower (making the experience as inconvenient as humanly possible).

Langen’s research found that deer crashes occur eight times more frequently per hour during dusk than during daylight and four times more frequently at dusk than after nightfall. Only about 20% of the collisions examined happened during daylight hours.

Fall is already the most dangerous time of year for deer-vehicle collisions, with the peak occurring around the rut.

According to the research highlighted by Wausau Pilot & Review, New York sees its highest number of deer-vehicle collisions during the last week of October and the first weeks of November. There are more than four times as many collisions during that period as there are in spring.

The fall time change can make that overlap even more significant.

The research found that the shift to standard time was associated with a 72% increase in deer-vehicle accidents during evening commuter hours, while morning commuter-hour crashes decreased by 37%. Overall, collisions during commuter hours increased 19% during the week following the fall time change in the New York data.

That doesn’t mean every driver will suddenly be more likely to hit a deer. But it does mean more vehicles can be traveling during the same twilight period when deer are particularly active.

What Drivers Should Know

Deer can cross roads at any time of day or night, but drivers should be especially alert around dawn and dusk and in areas where deer are common. The risk is also elevated during the fall rut, when bucks are actively searching for does and may move more frequently.

If a deer appears in the road, drivers should avoid swerving into another lane. Sudden lane changes can create a second collision with another vehicle, while hard braking may result in a rear-end crash.

The simplest approach is to slow down, stay alert and keep an eye on the roadside, particularly during the evening commute.

The clock change itself doesn’t make deer more active. Instead, it changes when people are driving, potentially putting more vehicles on the road during the same twilight hours when deer are naturally on the move.

And with the rut underway, that’s a combination drivers should take seriously.

2015 Patrick Stewart Thriller Just Became No. 1 Movie on Streaming

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Earlier this week, beloved actor Patrick Stewart died following his battle with Alzheimer’s Disease and Lewy Body Dementia.

“He has been held in the warm embrace of family and friends, surrounded by live music, prayer, tears, laughter and the kind of profound emotion that only he could inspire after a lifetime of devotion to us all,” publicist Bush Novak wrote in a statement. “We will forever cherish the peaceful ease and reflection on his face, an enduring reminder of his invincible grace.”

As fans mourn his passing, they’re reflecting on his legendary career by watching his seemingly endless catalogue of movies and TV shows. While he starred in a number high-profile superhero movies, it’s a more understated – and yet terrifying – role that fans are gravitating towards this weekend.

‘Green Room’ No. 1 on Streaming

The 2015 action, horror, thriller film Green Room starring Anton Yelchin and Imogen Poots, has been rising in the streaming ranks in the days since Stewart’s death. As of Saturday morning, it was the No. 1 movie on streaming, according to Just Watch.

“At the end of their low-budget tour, a punk band accepts a gig at an isolated rural club, only to discover that their hosts are a supremacist group,” reads the synopsis of the movie by Netflix.

The leader of that white supremacist group? None other than Patrick Stewart in a surprising twist for the audience, who grew up with Stewart’s Captain Jean-Luc Picard or Charles Xavier – also known as Professor X.

“Darcy Becker is a bad guy,” Stewart remarked in promo for the film. “He’s a really bad guy. And I have to be honest and say that I think it was that aspect that seduced me.

“I was sitting in my house in West Oxfordshire, and I settled down on a Saturday evening to read this script. I found it to be a page-turner! And it scared the [expletive] out of me. I was alone, in my house; at some point in the story I poured a large scotch, because it was so unsettling, that sense of being in a situation, surrounded by people you can’t reason with. If it does this to me, when it’s on screen, it might really have a lot of impact.”

Green Room owns an impressive 90% from critics and 75% from the audience on Rotten Tomatoes. The movie can be streamed on Netflix.
