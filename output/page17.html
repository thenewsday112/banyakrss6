Chainalysis Finds $443B MENA Crypto Economy as UAE Growth Outpaces Region

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

TL;DR: The United Arab Emirates was reported to have grown 32% to about $104.8 billion, while Türkiye remained larger at around $144.5 billion. The reporting period ends in June 2026, so its totals should not be presented as October market activity.

Chainalysis reported roughly $443.3 billion in cryptocurrency value received across the Middle East and North Africa during its July 2025–June 2026 study period, a decline of 1.4%. The study noted sharply different trajectories between individual countries.

The announcement and the numbers

The most concrete starting point is the dated disclosure from Chainalysis. It offers a specific basis for assessing Chainalysis’s 2026 Middle East and North Africa crypto adoption analysis without turning the story into unsupported price speculation.

The United Arab Emirates was reported to have grown 32% to about $104.8 billion, while Türkiye remained larger at around $144.5 billion. Chainalysis also pointed to a fivefold rise in Turkish domestic peer-to-peer stablecoin volume and interruptions to Iranian activity during internet blackouts.

The development arrives as digital-asset markets remain sensitive to funding conditions, infrastructure reliability and the gap between promotional claims and operational results. It warrants attention on its own facts, without assuming a particular cryptocurrency must rise or fall in response.

Why the development matters

The question for market participants is what Chainalysis’s 2026 Middle East and North Africa crypto adoption analysis establishes today and what remains uncertain. An immediate reaction is not the same as evidence of a durable economic outcome.

One regional average hides the different reasons people use cryptocurrency. Licensing can restructure the business landscape; inflation or currency access can influence stablecoin demand; network shutdowns can temporarily suppress the transactions analysts can observe.

Regional crypto usage reflects differences in banking access, remittances, regulation, inflation and technology. A rise in transaction value does not necessarily mean a proportional rise in individuals using digital assets to buy everyday goods.

Investors also need to separate changes in infrastructure from changes in token demand. Revenue may accrue to a company, a protocol treasury or a service provider without necessarily creating a one-for-one benefit for holders of a related token. Market impact depends on adoption, competition and the design of whatever fees or incentives are involved.

Risks the headline does not resolve

None of this removes the need for independent assessment. The published evidence identifies a development, while its risk and long-term economics are still open to examination.

On-chain measures capture transfers visible to analytical models, not every retail purchase or unique individual. Large institutional transactions, exchange transfers, currency hedging and remittances can all affect headline values.

The reporting period ends in June 2026, so its totals should not be presented as October market activity. Estimates are also sensitive to attribution methods and the range of services the analyst can identify on-chain.

When evaluating an adoption report, readers should examine the measurement period, what types of transfers are counted and how the analyst handles unidentified entities. Comparisons are most useful when the method is broadly consistent across countries.

The milestones to watch

The immediate checklist is relatively straightforward: look for verifiable implementation details and avoid interpreting a stated target as an outcome that has already occurred.

Future research should help distinguish durable merchant and remittance use from exchange churn, examine how new regulations affect licensed activity and track the resilience of payment channels during network disruptions.

A durable signal would be consistent follow-through: actual usage, transparent reporting and a credible explanation of risk. Short-term token-price moves can be driven by macroeconomic conditions or leverage and may say little about whether the product or initiative succeeds over months and years.

From a market perspective, the first task is to separate the scale of the announcement from the scale of economic activity that has actually occurred. A company may report a large addressable market, an institutional name or a future capability, but those are not equivalent to settled transactions or recurring fee income. This is why the primary disclosure is a better starting point than a trading narrative attached to it.

Primary source: Chainalysis’s primary announcement. This reporting reflects information available on October 31, 2026; targets, estimates and statements about future plans are attributed to their issuers.

Western Legend Clint Eastwood Turned Down No. 2 Action Movie of All Time

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Western legend Clint Eastwood was the top choice for a number of iconic roles – even once being targeted as the next James Bond.

But it was a choice in the late 1980s that helped create the career of a future action legend. Eastwood was the top choice to play reluctant hero cop John McClane in Die Hard, a role that eventually launched Bruce Willis into superstardom.

The Man With No Name series star allegedly turned down the film because he didn’t get the humor of the role, even though screenwriter Jeb Stuart claimed Eastwood was his inspiration for the role.

“They went to Clint Eastwood first. Ironically, his response to the producers was, ‘I don’t get the humor,”’ Stuart explained. “Which, for me, was a shock because if you listen to a lot of those words, Eastwood’s one of the few people who could have delivered a line like ‘Come to LA, have a great time.’ All that kind of stuff. You could see him doing that. He was my inspiration.”

Willis would later become an action legend, starring in roles like Armageddon and the underrated Red series.

‘Die Hard’ Ranked Among Best Action Movies

Studio Binder ranked Die Hard as the No. 2 action movie of all time just behind Mad Max: Fury Road.

“These days, there tends to be a trope in action movies that the hero needs to be a “chosen one” or is particularly special for some reason. Die Hard proves you don’t need any of that. The film follows a regular guy who just happens to be in the wrong place at the wrong time. He uses his wits and savvy, and as a result, the fights have a real weight to them because you know how vulnerable John McClane is,” Studio Binder wrote.

Die Hard owns an impressive 94% on Rotten Tomatoes from critics and audiences alike.

Henry Cavill and Antony Starr Are Among the Actors Who Auditioned for James Bond

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Last week, it was reported that Callum Turner, Jack Lowden, Josh O’Connor, and an unnamed fourth actor were undergoing screen tests amid Amazon MGM’s hunt for the new James Bond. While this may be the alleged shortlist to fill Daniel Craig’s shoes in the next Bond film, the British spy has been many an actor’s dream role for decades.

Back in 2005, a host of famous faces — some household names then, others gaining notoriety later in their career — auditioned for the Casino Royale remake, which would eventually see Craig cast in the role. Some of these screen tests leaked online, while other actors revealed they tried out for the part in their own admissions.

Henry Cavill

If you look at any social media post online about the ongoing Bond auditions, you can almost guarantee someone will mention Henry Cavill, in part thanks to his performances as Superman and The Witcher’s lead character, Geralt.

However, long before these roles, Cavill — then a 22-year-old emerging actor — was reportedly a finalist during auditions in 2005, with Casino Royale director Martin Campbell shortlisting him and inviting him for screen tests before the role eventually went to Craig.

Cavill’s age and physique likely played a part in his rejection. “I remember the director, Martin Campbell, saying, ‘Looking a little chubby there, Henry,’” Cavill said in an interview with Men’s Health. “I didn’t know how to train or diet. And I’m glad Martin said something, because I respond well to truth. It helps me get better.”

Dominic West

Dominic West at HBO’s “War” New York Premiere held at Perelman Performing Arts Center on September 22, 2026 in New York, New York. (Photo by John Nacion/Variety via Getty Images)

Shortly after becoming a star on the hit TV series The Wire, Dominic West tried out for another role with a massive following — 007.

 “At the time, I really wanted to get it. I love Bond, and I was the right age for it,” the actor said of his Casino Royale audition in a 2018 interview with Esquire. In the end, the War actor said he is now thankful he didn’t get the part.

“They asked me, ‘What do you think should happen with Bond?’ And I said something deeply uninspired like, ‘I think he should go back to being more like Sean Connery,'” West continued. “I thought then that it was the best job you can do. Now, I’m not so sure. You have a year-and-a-half of hell doing publicity.”

Antony Starr

Long before Antony Starr made a name for himself as the villain Homelander in The Boys, the Kiwi actor tried out for perhaps Britain’s best-loved hero (after Paddington Bear, of course).

When asked about his audition, which leaked online, by Josh Horowitz on the Happy Sad Confused podcast, Starr called his audition “One of the worst readings of ‘Bond. James Bond’ you’ll ever see.”

“My metaphor for that was like throwing a baitless hook into a very large pond, knowing you’re not gonna catch the fish,” he said. “They were just dragnetting the globe, and I was just one of the little minnows somewhere in there that got caught in the net. It was a bit surreal.”

“It came out of the blue, and I sort of went in and did that reading,” Starr continued. “It’s not very good, but it’s reflective of where I was at.”

Rupert Friend

VENICE, ITALY – SEPTEMBER 03: Rupert Friend attends the “Wild Horse Nine” Screening during the 83rd Venice International Film Festival at Sala Grande on September 03, 2026 in Venice, Italy. (Photo by Vittorio Zunino Celotto/Getty Images)

Fresh off his recent success as George Wickham in the 2005 version of Pride & Prejudice, Rupert Friend was one of countless British actors who threw their hat into the ring to audition for one of cinema’s most enduring figures.

In a 2023 interview with Variety, Friend said he was invited by the producers of Casino Royale to try out for Bond but ended up curtailing the process himself. “I just feel at this point in my life and career, I’m too young, I don’t have the experience, I don’t have the acting chops and I don’t have any of the hard knocks — emotionally, psychologically, physically — that a great Bond should have,” he said.

“So I’m gonna politely decline. That was probably a bit of an eyebrow raise for them.”

Sam Worthington

Before bagging lead roles in Avatar and Clash of the Titans, Sam Worthington auditioned for perhaps the most sought-after leading man role of them all.

Speaking to Variety in 2022, the Australian actor said he didn’t have the look for Bond — even after Bond producer Barbara Broccoli cut his hair during auditions. “I could play Bond as a killer, but I couldn’t get the debonair down for the life of me,” Worthington recalled. “The suit did not fit.” 

Matthew Rhys

LOS ANGELES, CALIFORNIA – SEPTEMBER 14: Matthew Rhys, winner of the Outstanding Lead Actor in a Comedy Series Award for “Widow’s Bay” and the Outstanding Lead Actor in a Limited or Anthology Series or Movie Award for “The Beast in Me” poses in the press room during the 78th Primetime Emmy Awards at Peacock Theater on September 14, 2026 in Los Angeles, California. (Photo by Kevin Mazur/Getty Images)

Matthew Rhys may have made history recently with his double win at the 78th Primetime Emmy Awards, thanks to his work in The Beast in Me and Apple TV’s Widow’s Bay, but one role evaded the Welshman entirely, despite his immense acting talent.

In an interview with The Times, Rhys said he was “terrified” when he went out for the part that eventually went to Craig. “They said, ‘What would you do differently with Bond?’ And I just remember going, ‘Ah,'” he recalled.

“I was so not anticipating that question. And then I was like, is it a trick question? Are they waiting for people to go, ‘I wouldn’t do anything. He’s perfect’?”

"Binance said it was able to replace the funds taken in the record hack by using company money to buy new tokens," reports NBC News: The remaining 10% consists of tokens that are less liquid, meaning they may take longer to buy, it said in a statement posted on X... Binance said it has set aside around $6.34 billion of company funds internally to cover the losses. The money spent re-buying tokens came from using part of its $1 billion Secure Asset Fund for Users, a bitcoin reserve it holds to protect customers in the event of security breaches, as well as some of its Futures Insurance Fund, and the company's own corporate treasury, it said on X. NBC News reports that the FBI blames the breach on a North Korea-sponsored group known as TraderTraitor: Members of [TraderTraitor] were observed by law enforcement converting stolen assets into the cryptocurrency ethereum, the FBI said in a statement, adding that it expects "further laundering" of the assets through the use of related technology... After the hack, the exchange stopped allowing customers to withdraw their funds, but it said Saturday it was still on track to reopen withdrawals Sunday. CNBC reports: The world's largest crypto exchange initially said the hack may involve "a compromised DevOps engineer's laptop, which allowed an attacker to gain access to a service container and submit unauthorized withdrawal transactions." However, Co-CEO Richard Teng later said the attack involved a more complex process rather than the misuse of a single engineer's permissions. The New York Times describes what happened when Binance first spotted "a multibillion-dollar deluge of digital coins" pouring out of its accounts: As the news spread, Binance's top executives put together an informal war room on Wea, the company's internal messaging system, with about 100 participants, including specialized cryptocurrency tracers and security personnel. They eventually transitioned to a mass conference call, with 100 boxes on the screen, where employees took turns muting and then unmuting, communicating in a mixture of English and Chinese. Among them was He Yi, Binance's co-chief executive, who is seven months pregnant, and worked through the night from her base in Abu Dhabi, taking a call at 3 a.m. local time.... Much of the drama played out in public. Almost all crypto transactions are recorded on the blockchain, a ledger of money movements that anyone can look up. That meant crypto analysts could see funds leaving Binance in real time... North Koreans have targeted crypto companies for years, using the proceeds of those thefts to fund its nuclear program, according to Western officials. The hackers have developed increasingly complex strategies to steal crypto and launder the proceeds, a painstaking endeavor that can take months to complete. In a blog post on Friday, Chainalysis said the attackers had deployed "laundering patterns we have not seen before," conducting thousands of transactions over a short period, probably with the help of automation. At the same time, Binance's colleagues in the industry mobilized to intercept some of the laundered crypto. Certain digital coin issuers can freeze funds, allowing them to halt a money-laundering operation in its tracks. Soon after the hack, Tether and Circle, two major issuers, announced that they had frozen stashes of their own coins that the hackers had seized from Binance, a combined total of more than $2 billion in crypto. Binance is now working to gain access to those funds, the company said. But $2.4 billion in crypto is already "beyond any issuer's reach," according to [blockchain intelligence firmm] TRM. The article also includes President Trump's reaction when asked Friday about the breach. "I've always felt it was a little bit dangerous. But they say they have it pretty much under control. We'll see."
