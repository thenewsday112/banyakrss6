Philippine Airlines Business Class Airbus A350 Review: Great Seats, But Missing Heart

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

For the long haul segment of the return portion of my first class quest to Japan, I flew Philippine Airlines’ Airbus A350-1000 business class on the 11hr55min flight from Manila (MNL) to San Francisco (SFO). I was very much looking forward to this flight, given that this is the carrier’s new flagship aircraft, featuring all-new cabins.

Ultimately my Philippine Airlines business class flight was good, especially when you account for value, given that Philippine Airlines tends to undercut the competition on price. If you can get over the awful ground experience in Manila, then there’s definitely value to be had here.

On the plus side, the cabin was brand new, and the food was pretty good. However, there were also some shortcomings, like the inoperable Wi-Fi, and there was maybe an x-factor missing with the soft product. Furthermore, while the crew was friendly enough, they didn’t have the warmth or attentiveness of the crew on my previous flight, as it felt like they were just going through the motions and disinterested.

So my conclusion is that this flight was a great deal, and I’d absolutely recommend this product if the price is right. But I feel like if Philippine Airlines tried a little harder, it could truly have a top tier product.

How I booked my Philippine Airlines business class ticket

I booked my return itinerary from Osaka to Manila to San Francisco as one ticket, with the first flight on the Airbus A330 (which I’ve already reviewed — that seat sucks!), and the second flight on the Airbus A350. I booked the following ticket, which cost $1,746.63:

9/19 PR411 Osaka to Manila departing 3:15PM arriving 6:40PM
9/19 PR104 Manila to San Francisco departing 11:15PM arriving 9:10PM

However, I ended up offsetting the cost of this ticket with points, so I really booked it with 116,442 Chase Ultimate Rewards points, because I could redeem for 1.5 cents each toward the cost of the ticket. That’s a reasonable paid fare, and in this case, I preferred redeeming points rather than having to spend the cash out of pocket.

Philippine Airlines business class lounge & boarding

In a previous installment, I reviewed the Philippine Airlines Mabuhay Lounge Manila, which is just awful, in my opinion. It was so bad that I went and sat out in the terminal, which is saying something, because the airport’s Terminal 1 makes JFK Terminal 1 (not the new one!) look like Singapore Changi, by comparison!

My flight was departing from gate five, with boarding scheduled for 10:30PM, 45 minutes before departure. All the US-bound flights from Manila depart from one area, with a centralized security check. It was quick for me, though obviously if you get “SSSS” on your boarding pass, you’ll get a more thorough inspection.

The gate area in this part of the terminal is just so poorly set up, as it’s a sea of seats, without many ways to easily pass through there. And as you’d expect, there are typically multiple flights departing at peak times, so it gets quite busy. There is a dedicated seating area for business class passengers, but there’s nothing actually special about that.

Philippine Airlines departure gate Manila Airport Philippine Airlines departure gate Manila Airport

Boarding ended up starting at 10:30PM, with dozens and dozens of wheelchairs passengers boarding first. Business class passengers were then invited to board around 15 minutes later.

Philippine Airlines A350 business class cabin & seats

I boarded through the forward left door, where I was greeted by the crew, and then turned right into the cabin. I’m usually really good at boarding first and getting pictures, but unfortunately by the time I boarded, around half of the business class passengers were seated (as wheelchair passengers get pre-boarding).

Philippine Airlines’ A350-1000s are equipped with the Collins Aerospace Elevation product, which is essentially the rebranding and slight evolution of the Super Diamond product, the world’s most popular reverse herringbone seat. Seats have 44″ of pitch, 21″ of width, and turn into 78″ flat beds.

The business class cabin consists of a total of 42 seats, spread across 11 rows, in a 1-2-1 configuration. This takes up the entire space between the first and second set of doors.

Philippine Airlines business class cabin A350

If you’re traveling with someone, you may want to select the pair of center seats, so that you can sit next to your companion. However, reverse herringbone seats aren’t ideal for this, since you do have to lean forward quite a bit to communicate with the person across from you. If you’re traveling alone and end up in a center seat, there’s also a privacy shield that you can extend between seats.

Philippine Airlines business class seats A350

If possible, solo travelers will of course want to select one of the window seats, since these offer the most privacy, and give you aisle access and a view. I assigned myself seat 10A, the window seat on the left side in the last row of the cabin.

Philippine Airlines business class seat A350

When it comes to comfort, these seats are excellent — they’re spacious, and offer great privacy and tech, as well as some storage.

Philippine Airlines business class seat A350

Next to the seat you’ll find a console, where there’s a wireless charging pad, as well as two storage compartments that can be opened.

Philippine Airlines business class seat console A350

One storage compartment is shallow and has a vanity, so this is ideal for storing a cell phone, passport, etc. The other storage compartment is a bit deeper, and has the entertainment controller, along with USB-A and USB-C outlets, and a headphone jack.

Philippine Airlines business class seat storage A350 Philippine Airlines business class seat charging A350

Next to the storage compartment is the small panel that can control all the seat functions, including seat recline, lighting, etc.

Philippine Airlines business class seat controls A350

In the back part of this area you’ll find the literature pocket, as well as a lamp that can be adjusted via a button at the top of it.

Philippine Airlines business class seat literature pocket A350

Underneath the seat and to the side you’ll find the AC power outlet, which is kind of hidden, unless you crouch over.

Philippine Airlines business class seat charging A350

In this seat, the entertainment monitor is fixed to the shell of the seat in front, and it’s a 20″ 4K UHD screen.

Philippine Airlines business class seat TV A350

The tray table in this seat folds out from underneath the entertainment monitor. When stored, it’s folded over in half, so to fully utilize it, you pull it out and then flip it over.

Philippine Airlines business class seat tray table A350

The seat has a good size ottoman, which becomes part of the bed when the seat is reclined.

Philippine Airlines business class seat ottoman A350

Then on the aisle-side of the seat there’s an armrest, and if you open it, there’s a little bit of storage, big enough for a water bottle.

Philippine Airlines business class seat armrest A350

Beyond that you’ll find the door, which can be opened and closed manually. Of course it’s locked during takeoff and landing, since it can only be used inflight.

Philippine Airlines business class seat door A350

The two other things worth mentioning about the cabin is that there are no individual air nozzles (grrr!), and there are also no overhead bins in the middle of the cabin. That makes the cabin feel more spacious, though it also means that there’s not quite as much storage space.

Philippine Airlines business class overhead console A350

All-in-all, Philippine Airlines has a really great hard product, so I have nothing but good things to say there. The seats are comfortable, the tech is great, and I appreciate that there are colors other than grey and black.

Philippine Airlines A350 business class entertainment & Wi-Fi

Philippine Airlines has audio and video on demand in its business class on the A350-1000. The airline has a solid selection of movies, TV shows, games, and audio. I couldn’t figure out exactly how many options there were of each, due to how they were organized. However, it was an extensive selection, though not to the level of what you’ll find on Emirates or Singapore Airlines.

Philippine Airlines business class entertainment A350 Philippine Airlines business class entertainment A350 Philippine Airlines business class entertainment A350 Philippine Airlines business class entertainment A350

Philippine Airlines business class entertainment A350

The system also had a map feature, as you’d expect. One thing that disappointed me is that there was no tail camera, as that’s something you’ll find on many A350 operators, and it’s something I really enjoy.

Philippine Airlines also offers bluetooth audio on its A350s, which is a handy feature.

In theory, Philippine Airlines’ A350-1000s are supposed to have Viasat Wi-Fi, which offers relatively high speed connectivity. However, it simply didn’t work on this flight. It’s not that it was intermittent or slow, it just wasn’t possible to connect.

It seems like one or two of the carrier’s A350s have non-functioning Wi-Fi. I understand tech can be complicated, but it’s also super frustrating when you board a long haul flight expecting to have connectivity, only to find out that it doesn’t work.

Philippine Airlines Wi-Fi system A350

Hopefully they get this resolved soon. On balance, the airline gets a thumbs down on entertainment based on my experience, since inflight Wi-Fi is something you expect as a standard nowadays on long haul flights.

Waiting at my Philippine Airlines business class seat upon boarding were a solid selection of amenities. There was a very plush pillow, a cozy blanket, and a mattress pad. While there was no luxury branding collaboration, the bedding was very good.

Philippine Airlines business class menu & drink list

Philippine Airlines business class pre-departure drink

All-in-all, the amenities were decent. I thought the bedding was very good, while the other amenities were just okay. There were no pajamas in business class, not that you’d necessarily expect them.

Philippine Airlines A350 departure from Manila

The boarding process on this flight was really drawn out, as every seat onboard was occupied. The entire boarding also took place through the forward left door, which was less than ideal in terms of foot traffic, but presumably that’s all that was possible at this gate.

Boarding took longer than planned, and the main cabin door finally closed at 11:30PM. Around that time the first officer was on the PA to inform us of our flight time of 11hr55min, and cruising altitude of 39,000 feet. A few moments later, the safety video was screened, which was rather entertaining (and dramatic).

At 11:35PM we began our pushback, and at 11:45PM we began our taxi. We only had a very short taxi out to our departure runway, and at 11:55PM we were cleared for takeoff on runway 6. We had a long roll down the runway, and then a smooth and gradual climb out.

The seatbelt sign was turned off just five minutes after takeoff.

For example, I was impressed by how everything was placed directly on the tray table, and the plating and presentation were quite nice. I also appreciated the pace of service, especially on a late night flight where many people are looking to sleep.

However, the food was just okay, and not great. There wasn’t a single dish I had where my expectations were exceeded, but instead, it all just tasted like relatively decent airplane food. I also feel like with the starter they could’ve added a salad, or something.

Philippine Airlines A350 business class lavatory

After the meal, I checked out the lavatory on the Philippine Airlines A350-1000. There were two located at the front of the cabin, which were reserved for business class. There were also two behind the cabin, but they were for premium economy. The lavatories were totally standard for an A350, and were modern without being overly spacious.

Philippine Airlines A350 business class bed

Philippine Airlines business class crew & service

Filipinos are known for being incredibly warm and hospitable, and Philippine Airlines always talks about how it’s “the heart of the Filipino.” On my previous (short) flight, I noted how the crew was incredibly friendly — they were genuinely warm, hospitable, and attentive. On this flight, I didn’t get the same impression.

Don’t get me wrong, the crew wasn’t rude, but they just lacked all the things I noted about the previous crew. There was definitely a lack of passion, they felt rushed, and they weren’t as attentive in interactions. I hate to nitpick, but let me give some general examples…

For one, the purser never came around to individually greet each passenger, or even check on them in any form, unlike on my previous flight. My only interaction with her was that apparently the million miler in the cabin could deplane before everyone else, so she’d yell at all other passengers “excuse me, million miler, million miler.”

The crew just also felt like they were going through the motions with no attentiveness. Not once did I see a flight attendant walking down the aisle between meals to see if anyone wanted anything (though when I pushed the call button, it was quickly answered). For example, when you’re served a midflight snack, you’d think they’d check on you after 10 minutes to see if it’s done, if they could clear the dish, etc. In this case, I had to push the call button for it to be removed.

I also just felt like they weren’t actually that nice, especially in terms of how they communicated. For example, when I asked for a cheese plate as well with the first meal, the answer was simply “I will check,” rather than “of course, if we have any left I will get it for you,” or something along those lines. Similarly, when I ordered the midflight snack, the response was simply “let me see if we have that.”

Of course these are all minor things, but I think the way I’d sum up my general interactions with this crew is that it felt like passengers were an inconvenience, rather than guests they were happy to have onboard.

Philippine Airlines flight wheelchairs San Francisco Airport

Bottom line

I’m happy I had the chance to fly Philippine Airlines’ new flagship Airbus A350-1000 business class. In terms of hard product, this is a huge upgrade over what the airline has offered in the past, with excellent business class suites with doors, and great tech.

The catch is that the soft product left a bit to be desired. The Viasat Wi-Fi was broken the entire flight, the cappuccinos promised on the menu don’t exist, and I thought the crew lacked warmth, especially compared to my previous flight. And all of that says nothing of the experience of connecting through Manila Airport, which is unpleasant, to put it politely.

The thing is, ultimately I’d fly with the airline again if the price is right, since you really can’t argue with Philippine Airlines’ business class pricing. If the price and schedule work, it’s definitely an attractive option. I just feel like if the airline invested a bit more in its product, it would truly be competitive with top carriers, and wouldn’t have to rely on discounting so heavily in order to fill cabins.

Hirst, 27, has struggled for form so far for the Potters though and hasn’t made an immediate impact for Mark Robins’ side. He has made 12 appearances in all competitions this season, 11 of which have come in the Championship, and he has chipped in with one goal and one assist.

Hinchcliffe, who played for Manchester City, Everton and Sheffield Wednesday, has delivered his honest verdict on the player, as per Sky Sports: “George Hirst is one player who hasn’t really got going yet. He’s someone who could come to the fore a bit more as well.”

Stoke City drew 0-0 at home against Bristol City last time out. They are back in action this weekend on their own patch again against Preston North End.

Stoke City slow start for summer signing George Hirst

Stoke City paid big money to snap up Hirst from Ipswich Town. Therefore, he is under pressure to perform at SCFC.

The Potters acquired him to bolster their attacking department. He provides them with more competition and depth at the top end of the pitch.

Hirst, who is from Sheffield, battles it out with the likes of Lamine Cisse, Robert Bozenik and Sam Gallagher for a place in Robins’ starting XI. As Hinchcliffe has alluded to, he isn’t a guaranteed starter at the moment.

Oklahoma's SB 1589 makes running or supporting a dual-currency sweepstakes casino a Class C2 felony from November 1, after lawmakers overrode the governor's veto. Its reach into payment, geolocation and affiliate businesses raises the legal stakes for the whole sweepstakes supply chain.

Oklahoma’s sweepstakes casino ban starts on November 1, and operators are not waiting for the date. In recent days, brands such as High 5, McLuck, Hello Millions, SpinBlitz, PlayFame, Zula, Sportzino and Fortune Wins have either left the state or blocked its players.

The law’s reach down the supply chain sets it apart. Liability extends beyond operators to suppliers, payment processors, geolocation providers and affiliates. Tokens and similar currencies used in dual-currency models count as “representatives of value.” Tribal gaming approved under the federal Indian Gaming Regulatory Act is exempt.

Lawmakers overrode Governor Stitt to pass the ban

Senator Todd Gollihare authored SB 1589. It had its first Senate reading on February 2, 2026. On March 25, 2026, Representative Kyle Hilbert replaced Representative Fetgatter as its principal House author. The measure changes the scope of an existing gambling offense in the state’s crimes and punishments code.

Stitt’s veto letter objected that the bill needlessly created a new felony. He also opposed extending criminal liability to businesses and service providers. In his veto message, he argued that Oklahoma’s gaming laws should be clear, targeted and fair. SB 1589, he said, did not meet that standard.

The legislature disagreed. In the Senate, Gollihare moved that the bill become law despite the veto. The motion passed on a roll-call vote. The House then passed the measure over the veto by two-thirds of its elected members. That threshold is set by Article VI, Section 11 of the state constitution.

The provisions the governor objected to are now the ones that matter most for the industry. Businesses that serve sweepstakes brands, from payment firms to geolocation vendors, now face felony exposure in the state. That raises the legal risk across the sector, not just for the brands themselves.

Players face a different problem. The rules on remaining balances and pending redemptions are unclear. Oklahoma has no regulated online casino. As a result, legal online play in the state now goes only to tribal gaming.

Wyoming and Pennsylvania are considering similar bans

Other states are moving in the same direction. Pennsylvania has folded a sweepstakes ban into a broader gaming bill, HB 2801. Each violation by a sweepstakes operator could draw a fine from regulators if that bill passes. Pending bills in Pennsylvania do not carry over into 2027.

Wyoming’s draft would ban online casino and sweepstakes games under the state’s professional gambling law. The Select Committee on Gaming sent draft bill 27LSO-0098 to the Legislature’s Management Council. The council will decide whether to sponsor it in the 2027 session. If enacted, the Wyoming ban would take effect on July 1, 2027.
