The Philadelphia Eagles made a few final roster moves ahead of their Sunday Night Football clash with the Washington Commanders, adding depth at running back and linebacker while losing another cornerback.

Philadelphia elevated running back Elijah Mitchell and linebacker Mason Reiger from the practice squad, according to ESPN’s Adam Schefter. Shortly after, cornerback Jakorian Bennett was downgraded to out, per Jeff McLane of The Philadelphia Inquirer.

Bennett had been dealing with a knee injury this week, though he was a full participant in practice on Wednesday and Thursday. His absence leaves the Eagles even thinner in the secondary, as fellow cornerback Jonathan Jones had already been ruled out with a hamstring injury.

Mitchell’s elevation provides some insurance in the backfield. Saquon Barkley returned to practice on a limited basis Friday after missing time with hamstring and back injuries, and he is expected to be available against Washington. Still, the Eagles appear to want an extra option in case Barkley is limited.

Mitchell rejoined Philadelphia’s practice squad on Oct. 20, when the team also waived running backs Jaydon Blue and Carson Steele. The Commanders have had their own struggles, sitting at 1-4-1 entering the matchup. The Eagles beat Washington 24-22 in Week 1, and they will look to complete the season sweep at Northwest Stadium while snapping their losing streak.

More than a sidekick! Bundesliga youngster must escape endless comparisons

"I won't make it to the very top of world football. My ceiling was lower—but that ceiling was still the Bundesliga," Felix Kroos wrote about the endless comparisons with his brother Toni. Your own achievements do not become any less significant just because someone else reaches even greater heights. Malek El Mala would do well to remember those words as he potentially makes his Bundesliga debut for Köln today.

The 21-year-old is also living in someone else's shadow. Dismissed as the brother you simply have to sign for the youth team if you want to get your hands on Saïd. That was the story circulating in the summer, when talk turned to the astronomical fee last Bundesliga season's breakout star might be sold for.

Malek El Mala, the add-on. That was how he was seen for a long time, but now he is on the verge of stepping onto football's biggest stage himself. He has earned it in his own right. Given the constant pressure to deliver results, no professional coach can be accused of handing out squad places lightly.

El Mala has already been named in the squad for two first-team matches, but has yet to come off the bench. That could change today against Union Berlin, his coach René Wagner hinted at the press conference: "In Malek, we have an out-and-out number nine in the squad who will have a role to play in games where we need that specific position."

He is benefiting from Köln's injury situation. Ragnar Ache, Thijs Dallinga and Imad Rondic are all out, opening the door to the first team. But that is nothing unusual for young players: most benefit at some point from established options being unavailable through injury or a dip in form.

📸 Christof Koepsel - 2025 Getty Images

An opportunity he earned himself

Even so, you have to put yourself forward at the right moment to get your chance. El Mala has scored five goals in eleven Regionalliga appearances. Most recently, he netted three times in two matches. In other words, he has put himself in a position to become an option for Wagner.

He is still very young. Twenty-one. A good age to develop and take your first steps in professional football. For Wagner, the reason it has not happened sooner is obvious: "We all know Malek's story. He's had a few injury problems along the way."

But now the breakthrough is supposed to come: "Malek is knocking ever louder on the door," Wagner said, according to 'Express', after last Tuesday's cup victory, which also came against Union Berlin. The coach also believes in a successful future for him: "We mustn't forget that Malek is still at the beginning of his career. If he keeps this up, his time will definitely come. Perhaps very soon."

Not everyone can take off the way Saïd El Mala did last season. Many young players need more patience and have to work hard for their playing time. Breaking free of comparisons with his hugely successful brother and appreciating his own career will be key for Malek.

Measuring himself against himself

He can learn from Felix Kroos here: "Something else was harder for me: at times, I started to belittle my own career and achievements," he wrote in a piece for 'Sports Illustrated'. "Even within my family, I sometimes felt that my success wasn't appreciated as much as it might have been without Toni's extraordinary career. That made me sad."

He said he had needed to remind himself that he had played 250 professional matches, and what an extraordinary achievement that was. A milestone that will forever remain a dream for many. "In the end, though, I had to look in the mirror and find my own yardstick. If someone had outlined the course of my career to me when I was 16, I would have signed up for it straight away," Kroos said.

That is what it is about for Malek El Mala, too: focusing on himself, following his own path and appreciating how hard it is simply to earn a place in a Bundesliga squad and be able to dream of making your debut. It is about fighting for every appearance so that, when he finally hangs up his boots, he has made the most of his abilities.

Joan Garcia talks Farinas, high defensive line, Atletico Madrid and more after Barcelona 6-1 Alaves

FC Barcelona came back to winning ways on Saturday evening thanks to a resounding performance that saw them secure a 6-1 victory over Deportivo Alaves.

The dominant night saw Lamine Yamal and Raphinha both score a brace, though Barcelona goalkeeper Joan Garcia will be disappointed not to have retained a clean sheet, as Alaves’ Mariano Diaz scored a late goal.

Speaking to the media after the game, Garcia talked about the response to a difficult week, his makeshift defence and the fine margins that cost them against Europe’s elite.

Garcia’s view on Barcelona performance

Garcia was delighted with the manner of the bounce-back, even though a late goal denied him a clean sheet.

It was a dominant display from Barcelona. (Photo by Alex Caparros/Getty Images)

“We did our job. We were focused and played well. We had to respond after the tough week we had with two defeats. We’re still top of the league,” he said.

“We have to keep a cool head to stay in first place. As for the goal we conceded? Every goalkeeper will tell you the same thing, that they want a clean sheet, but the important thing is to win,” he added.

Garcia will take the result, especially after a trying weekend against Real Madrid, where he conceded two goals.

Praise for de Jong and Farinas

The goalkeeper reserved special mention for Frenkie de Jong and Brian Farinas, both of who also received praise from Hansi Flick for their strong performances in rather unfamiliar roles.

“It was a somewhat unusual defensive line for us. But Frenkie and Brian showed great character and demonstrated how good they are as players. Brian had a very good game in a difficult match because it wasn’t his usual position,” he said (h/t Mundo Deportivo).

“He showed the personality he has. And Frenkie gives us a lot on the ball; he almost never loses it. Good players can perform well regardless of their position. That’s exactly what they both showed today. I’m very happy for them,” he added.

Lots of learning

When asked about the recent defeats to PSG and Real Madrid, Garcia pinpointed small details as the difference.

“Against these great teams like PSG and Real Madrid, everything comes down to details at the end. Here in the last match against Madrid, we did many things well, but in the end, it all came down to two details.

Barcelona will be more confident for games ahead. (Photo by Alex Caparros/Getty Images)

“We must focus on that, on maintaining concentration, avoiding mistakes, because we know that we have a lot of potential up front, and if we are a reliable and solid team, we have a much better chance of winning,” he said.

Despite those results, he was adamant the team has improved defensively, while also defending the high line of press that was criticized widely after the Real Madrid defeat.

“We have taken a step forward and I think we are conceding far fewer chances than last year, but we still need to polish a couple of things.

“It’s also our style of play; we know we have that risk and we have shown that it’s worth taking it. It’s true that you are obviously a bit more exposed, but I think that in attack it’s worth taking those risks,” he said.

Finally, with a Champions League meeting with Aston Villa and a trip to Atletico on the horizon, Garcia is excited for the coming weeks.

“We’re really looking forward to what’s coming up. It’s been great to rest a bit more this week and win this match. And the way it went also helps a lot.

“Now, we’re thinking about resting, preparing for the next Champions League match here at home, and we’re really looking forward to it and playing a good game,” he concluded.

British baseball champions crowned a century after win

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →
Fred Lewis was the driving force behind Chipping Norton Baseball Club [Chipping Norton Museum]

It's America's national pastime, but during the 1920s it was a small town in the Cotswolds that was flying the flag for baseball in the UK.

At the same time Babe Ruth was leading the New York Yankees to the top of the sport across the pond, Chipping Norton were being crowned British champions in back-to-back years.

But in a wait that's taken longer than the Red Sox needed to break the Curse of the Bambino, the Cotswold market town's success had never been officially recognised.

Now, a century on from the team's first triumph, the championship has finally been certified by the British Baseball Federation - with a plaque celebrating the team recently unveiled at a local museum.

At the forefront of 1926 Chipping Norton baseball team was its founder Fred Lewis - a local undertaker with a fine sporting pedigree.

"He didn't like cricket, he didn't like football, but otherwise he was a great sportsman. He played a very good game of tennis and golf," his granddaughter Jill Kelly said.

"But he just absolutely loved baseball, he really did."

Although most people associate baseball with the USA and its major league - the game was in fact first played on the landed estates of Surrey.

But it was in the US that the game was first truly associated and organised. Over the years, it has gone on to become just about as American as apple pie.

In the early 1900s, it was Lewis who brought the game to the rolling hills of the Cotswolds - founding the Chipping Norton Baseball Club after seeing baseball in a sporting pamphlet.

Bill Williams, a former PE teacher who now volunteers at Chipping Norton Museum, said Lewis was "just a man who had a finger in every Chipping Norton pie".

"In the early days, they had very little equipment, but he designed and, as far as I'm aware, made the kit that the [baseball] teams played in."

"He did do other things, obviously, but I think it was very much the centre of his life."

The Chipping Norton team of 1926 won the national championship at London's Stamford Bridge [Chipping Norton Museum]

By the roaring 1920s, Lewis had adopted the Field of Dreams motto "If you build it, they will come" and turned Chipping Norton into a hotbed of British baseball.

"By all accounts, they would attract large crowds on a Sunday afternoon - sometimes two, three hundred people would come along," Williams said.

In 1926, Chipping Norton beat the London Americans at Stamford Bridge, now more famously known as the home of Chelsea FC - to win their first national title.

A local newspaper report from the time said Lewis had told his defeated opponents that the secret of his team's success was "in their blood".

The club would retain the title the following year, but Williams said "things started to fade as Fred got older".

"When he died in 1960 at age 81, the club sort of fizzled out."

Neither of their national titles had ever been officially recognised by what would go on to be the governing body of the sport in the UK, British Baseball.

Jill Kelly, granddaughter of Fred Lewis, said he would have been "so pleased" with the recognition [BBC]

That was until earlier this month, after Williams led a years-long campaign to have the Chipping Norton club's achievements officially acknowledged.

But that changed last Saturday, as Williams and Kelly unveiled a plaque at Chipping Norton Museum to celebrate the success. It was approved by British Baseball.

"[It's] fantastic, I'm so chuffed that we finally got to do this," Williams said.

"There was a point where I thought this might never happen, but thankfully the British Baseball Federation have now officially recognised Chippy."

"I suppose it is [mission accomplished] really."

Kelly, who is in her 90s and now lives in Tewkesbury, said her grandfather Fred Lewis would have been "so pleased" with the recognition.

"He'd be absolutely delighted that the game had been recognised, mind you, you wouldn't have been able to stop him talking about it."
