Reading a mobile game screen pixel by pixel

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Clash of Clans gives you 30 seconds to look at a base before the attack starts. That's when you figure out where the loot is, where to go in, where you can even drop troops. I wanted a model that could look at that screen for me.

Couldn't find a dataset for it. So I built one.

What's actually on the screen

More than you'd think. Buildings, walls, traps, rocks, trees, decorations, troops, heroes, the army bar at the bottom, loot and timer at the top. And everything overlaps. Decorations sit in front of buildings, the UI sits on top of the base.

So every object is a polygon of only the pixels you can actually see. If a statue covers half a cannon, the cannon's mask is just the half that shows.

Classes:

  • Buildings with their level, because a level 10 cannon and a level 15 cannon don't look alike
  • Walls by shape (leaning left, leaning right, joined)
  • Troops, heroes, the cards in the army bar
  • UI and HUD text

That ends up at 1,912 classes.

Before, during, after

A base looks pretty different depending on when you grab the screen, so I kept three scenes:

Scene Frames Scout screen, before the battle 5,496 During the battle 1,316 End of battle 97

6,909 frames, around 4.2M masks. YOLO seg and COCO.

The val set is split by base. Same base in train and val and your model just memorises it, and your numbers lie to you. None of the val bases are in training.

First thing I trained on it

A deploy zone model. Every base has a red border you can't drop troops inside, and on crowded bases it's honestly hard to see.

The model predicts the no-deploy area as a mask and I trace the border from that. Plain U-Net, two sizes: MobileNetV2 (27 MB) and EfficientNet-B4 (78 MB). The bigger one also learns the UI as its own class so the mask stops leaking onto the army bar.

0.996 IoU on the no-deploy area on val.

Joan Garcia talks Farinas, high defensive line, Atletico Madrid and more after Barcelona 6-1 Alaves

FC Barcelona came back to winning ways on Saturday evening thanks to a resounding performance that saw them secure a 6-1 victory over Deportivo Alaves.

The dominant night saw Lamine Yamal and Raphinha both score a brace, though Barcelona goalkeeper Joan Garcia will be disappointed not to have retained a clean sheet, as Alaves’ Mariano Diaz scored a late goal.

Speaking to the media after the game, Garcia talked about the response to a difficult week, his makeshift defence and the fine margins that cost them against Europe’s elite.

Garcia’s view on Barcelona performance

Garcia was delighted with the manner of the bounce-back, even though a late goal denied him a clean sheet.

It was a dominant display from Barcelona. (Photo by Alex Caparros/Getty Images)

“We did our job. We were focused and played well. We had to respond after the tough week we had with two defeats. We’re still top of the league,” he said.

“We have to keep a cool head to stay in first place. As for the goal we conceded? Every goalkeeper will tell you the same thing, that they want a clean sheet, but the important thing is to win,” he added.

Garcia will take the result, especially after a trying weekend against Real Madrid, where he conceded two goals.

Praise for de Jong and Farinas

The goalkeeper reserved special mention for Frenkie de Jong and Brian Farinas, both of who also received praise from Hansi Flick for their strong performances in rather unfamiliar roles.

“It was a somewhat unusual defensive line for us. But Frenkie and Brian showed great character and demonstrated how good they are as players. Brian had a very good game in a difficult match because it wasn’t his usual position,” he said (h/t Mundo Deportivo).

“He showed the personality he has. And Frenkie gives us a lot on the ball; he almost never loses it. Good players can perform well regardless of their position. That’s exactly what they both showed today. I’m very happy for them,” he added.

Lots of learning

When asked about the recent defeats to PSG and Real Madrid, Garcia pinpointed small details as the difference.

“Against these great teams like PSG and Real Madrid, everything comes down to details at the end. Here in the last match against Madrid, we did many things well, but in the end, it all came down to two details.

Barcelona will be more confident for games ahead. (Photo by Alex Caparros/Getty Images)

“We must focus on that, on maintaining concentration, avoiding mistakes, because we know that we have a lot of potential up front, and if we are a reliable and solid team, we have a much better chance of winning,” he said.

Despite those results, he was adamant the team has improved defensively, while also defending the high line of press that was criticized widely after the Real Madrid defeat.

“We have taken a step forward and I think we are conceding far fewer chances than last year, but we still need to polish a couple of things.

“It’s also our style of play; we know we have that risk and we have shown that it’s worth taking it. It’s true that you are obviously a bit more exposed, but I think that in attack it’s worth taking those risks,” he said.

Finally, with a Champions League meeting with Aston Villa and a trip to Atletico on the horizon, Garcia is excited for the coming weeks.

“We’re really looking forward to what’s coming up. It’s been great to rest a bit more this week and win this match. And the way it went also helps a lot.

“Now, we’re thinking about resting, preparing for the next Champions League match here at home, and we’re really looking forward to it and playing a good game,” he concluded.

KRÜ Blaze’s lower bracket run reaches three wins

Saturday’s victory was KRÜ Blaze’s third successive series win in the lower bracket. Both earlier wins were sweeps. They beat Team Liquid Brazil 2-0 in the quarter-finals, then dispatched MIBR GC by the same score in the semi-finals.

That run means they have won seven of the eight maps played since dropping out of the upper bracket. They also came into the event as winners of the Latin America regional final, where they beat Akave Esports Black 3-1.

Rotterdam studio The New Raw has used discarded rope salvaged from the city’s harbour to create a series of 3D-printed public seating for its waterfront.

Called Buoy, the urban furniture project is shortlisted for a 2026 Dezeen Award in the sustainable design category.

To create the stools, The New Raw founder Foteini Setaki collected a series of plastic ropes from Rotterdam harbour, which were formerly used for managing boats and would've otherwise gone to waste.

The ropes were then transported to Maastricht, in the south of the Netherlands, where they were cleaned, shredded and mechanically recycled into pellets.

After returning to The New Raw's Rotterdam studio, the recycled plastic pellets were melted and 3D-printed to create the stools, layer by layer.

"The material fuses as it is deposited, so no separate binder is needed," Setaki told Dezeen.

Setaki explained that marine ropes are challenging to recycle because they contain a cocktail of materials that accumulate various residues over time.

Careful selection is essential to determine the ropes with the highest polypropylene content to be transformed into recycled plastic pellets, she explained.

While no virgin plastic is used in the creation of the stools, a small percentage of additives support the furniture's long-term outdoor use, including UV protection.

"These additives are compatible with recycling the material again," said Setaki.

Each seat features a subtly different hue, which is determined by the colour of the original rope rather than additional pigments or coatings.

The stools were also designed with spinning top-style pointed bases, so that they rock when the user sits down.

This gentle rocking motion was chosen to mimic real buoys bobbing along on the water.

"We wanted the seats to carry a recognisable link to their maritime origin, through both their shape and the slow movement of floating buoys," explained Setaki.

"Borrowing that movement brings a playful quality to sitting, and the connection to the harbour becomes something you can feel."

Each unique stool was engraved with its batch and production number, and then placed by Rotterdam harbour.

The project was created as an example of how designers can seek out local, surplus resources in an effort to contextualise their practice with the place that they work.

"Working with a local waste stream is a way of strengthening the relationship between the city and its harbour," said Setaki.

"The material's journey and the experience of sitting make that relationship tangible. This approach could also be adapted to other harbour cities, starting with their own discarded materials and local networks."

Founded in 2016, The New Raw is run by Setaki and Panos Sakkas. The studio 3D prints locally sourced plastic waste to create furniture.

Buoy is nominated alongside a vibrant shortlist of sustainable design projects, including a cement-free architectural material made of waste date pits and lighting for an Australia bookshop made of its own faulty publications rejected due to printing errors.

The Lakers’ loss to the Golden State Warriors on Friday will certainly be in the running for one of their worst defeats this season. When it comes to games like this, the best thing to do is take note of what went wrong and delete the tape, especially after that first quarter.

Three things went wrong for the purple and gold in that game. For starters, they struggled mightily whenever Luka Dončić was on the bench. Second, they committed a season-high 20 turnovers, with Austin Reaves accounting for eight. Lastly, the Warriors simply played harder and smarter than the Lakers.

The Lakers can’t be outworked once again if they want to win this game.

It would be nice to see a better performance from Reaves and Walker Kessler who has been quite mediocre for his standards over the last couple of games. Reaves and Dončić will face a lot of ball pressure and physicality, so they’ll have to counter with their ability to manipulate the defense and control the game’s pace.

After suffering their first loss of the season against the Portland Trail Blazers, the Lakers wasted no time responding, coming out firing and cruising past the L.A. Clippers the following day. Now, the Lakers have another opportunity to bounce back from their worst loss of the young season. It’ll be interesting to see how they respond and what adjustments they make moving forward.

Let’s see if they can execute better and more importantly, get back on the winning column against the Raptors on Sunday.
