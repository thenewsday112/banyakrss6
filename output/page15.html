The Cleveland Cavaliers dominated the Milwaukee Bucks inside, leading to a runaway 136-111 victory.

Three stars of the game

First star: Jarrett Allen

25 points, 11 rebounds, 4 assists, 1 steal

Allen was an absolute force to be reckoned with. He relentlessly attacked the basket by rolling hard to the rim and looking for any opening he could find to make himself available for a pass inside. Defensively, he controlled the paint and cleaned up the glass.

This was the ideal game from Allen.

Second star: Evan Mobley

27 points, 5 rebounds, 3 assists, 1 steal

Basically everything I said about Allen could also be said of Mobley. He was aggressive offensively, attacking inside. And when the defense sagged off, he took the outside shot, going 3-for-5 from beyond the arc.

This game was the template from a process and results standpoint for Mobley.

Third star: James Harden

13 points, 7 assists, 6 rebounds

Harden spread the ball around and looked to get the bigs involved. It was exactly what the Cavs needed from their starting point guard.

Dud of the game

Bum: Tyler Herro

15 points, 3 rebounds, 2 assists

Milwaukee’s point-of-attack defense was a mess. Herro wasn’t the only reason to blame for that, but he was the most noteworthy example of someone who wasn’t upholding their end of the bargain. On top of that, Herro struggled to make anything happen as a scorer as he went just 5-for-18 from the field.

Cavaliers who deserve shoutouts

Donovan Mitchell

15 points, 3 assists, 4 rebounds, +31

Mitchell let the game come to him in a way that he doesn’t always do. He attacked when the moment called for it, and he deferred to his teammates when they had cleaner looks. This led to Mitchell controlling the game without controlling the ball, making room for the dominant performances from the bigs.

Mario Hezonja

8 points, 11 rebounds, 4 assists

Hezonja thinks the game at a high level. He does a great job of reading how the opponent is playing, and then exploiting it to the best of his ability. In this game, that weakness was rebounding.

Milwaukee had to send extra bodies to contain Mobley and Allen on both ends. Hezonja ducked inside around the help to grab 11 rebounds, including four on the offensive end.

Quote of the day

“It was part of our film session this morning, so it’s nice to see it transfer. Reward these guys, they’re both playing great. Sixteen shot attempts, we were very intentional about getting them the ball. Good job by our perimeters, finding those guys in the right spots.”

Stat of the game

Jarrett Allen and Evan Mobley led the team with 16 shot attempts each.

Allen and Mobley were aggressive in looking for opportunities to score, and their teammates were consistent in getting them the ball. That is a formula worth repeating.

This game will be remembered for how the Cavs got the bigs involved

Mobly and Allen were forces all night. Not just for a few minutes, not just for a quarter, but until the game was completely out of reach early in the fourth quarter. The bigs stayed aggressive inside, and their teammates kept feeding them the ball.

Getting the bigs involved to this degree can destroy a defense. It’s similar to establishing the run in football. Once you control the line of scrimmage, the pass game opens up because the defense has to sell out to overload the box.

Milwaukee’s defense tried to shrink the floor to stop Mobley and Allen. Defenders crashed down from the corners in an effort to cut off the initial drive, but this opened up the floor for everyone, including whatever big wasn’t the main focus of the defense. This allowed for some of the big-to-big passes and put-back offensive rebounds.

This offense is at its best when they’re forcing the ball to the bigs, and the bigs are rolling hard and aggressively looking for openings to attack inside. Despite that, both groups have struggled to establish the bigs as consistent scoring threats for a full 48 minutes.

Far too often, the Cavs abandon this formula when things get tough. Either the guards take it upon themselves to handle the majority of the scoring load or the bigs become passive and stop looking for opportunities inside — and usually it’s both.

It’s not always this easy to establish the bigs as scorers. Milwaukee’s defense is bad and easy to exploit, but this formula should be more repeatable than it’s proven to be. Maybe this game can set them on the right path.

Polygon Adds Agent Payment Limits to Open Money Stack

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

TL;DR: Polygon described use cases such as software agents buying API calls, compute capacity or digital services without seeking a fresh human signature for every permitted small purchase. Policy settings cannot eliminate all risks.

Polygon Labs detailed agent payment controls for its Open Money Stack on October 21. The feature lets an application authorize a defined payment job while restricting an automated agent to an approved stablecoin budget, specified actions and an expiration time.

The announcement and the numbers

The most concrete starting point is the dated disclosure from Polygon Labs. It offers a specific basis for assessing Polygon’s controls for AI-agent stablecoin spending without turning the story into unsupported price speculation.

Polygon described use cases such as software agents buying API calls, compute capacity or digital services without seeking a fresh human signature for every permitted small purchase. The wallet checks requests against the permissions set by the business.

Announcements in crypto often arrive before the data needed to price their longer-term significance. A sensible reading separates what has already been delivered from what still needs commercial adoption, technical validation or regulatory clearance.

Why the development matters

The question for market participants is what Polygon’s controls for AI-agent stablecoin spending establishes today and what remains uncertain. An immediate reaction is not the same as evidence of a durable economic outcome.

Payment control is emerging as a practical bottleneck for agentic commerce. Networks may process tiny transactions quickly, but businesses need an audit trail and a clear answer when an automated system makes a wrong or unexpected purchase.

Automated or stablecoin-based payments are also exposed to ordinary commercial risks: incorrect recipients, operational downtime, liquidity shortages and inconsistent compliance coverage. New rails need dependable safeguards, not just faster transaction confirmation.

A durable signal would be consistent follow-through: actual usage, transparent reporting and a credible explanation of risk. Short-term token-price moves can be driven by macroeconomic conditions or leverage and may say little about whether the product or initiative succeeds over months and years.

Risks the headline does not resolve

None of this removes the need for independent assessment. The published evidence identifies a development, while its risk and long-term economics are still open to examination.

An agent that can make purchases needs limited authority, not unrestricted control of an account. Spending caps, approved counterparties and time limits narrow the damage a mistaken instruction or compromised agent might cause.

Policy settings cannot eliminate all risks. A sufficiently broad authorization can still allow unwanted payments, while agents may misinterpret vendor identity, invoice purpose or terms even when every requested transfer fits a numerical limit.

The ‘last mile’ of a digital payment is often its most complicated component. A blockchain transfer may settle quickly, yet the recipient still needs usable money, and the provider must reconcile records, comply with local rules and support failures or reversals.

The milestones to watch

The immediate checklist is relatively straightforward: look for verifiable implementation details and avoid interpreting a stated target as an outcome that has already occurred.

Developers should test revocation, monitoring, transaction reconciliation and failure behavior before deploying the controls in production. Real-world volume and documented incident handling will indicate whether the system is robust enough for business use.

From a market perspective, the first task is to separate the scale of the announcement from the scale of economic activity that has actually occurred. A company may report a large addressable market, an institutional name or a future capability, but those are not equivalent to settled transactions or recurring fee income. This is why the primary disclosure is a better starting point than a trading narrative attached to it.

Investors also need to separate changes in infrastructure from changes in token demand. Revenue may accrue to a company, a protocol treasury or a service provider without necessarily creating a one-for-one benefit for holders of a related token. Market impact depends on adoption, competition and the design of whatever fees or incentives are involved.

What is Connections?

The NYT's latest daily word game has become a social media hit. The Times credits associate puzzle editor Wyna Liu with helping to create the new word game and bringing it to the publications' Games section. Connections can be played on both web browsers and mobile devices and require players to group four words that share something in common.

If a player gets all four words in a set correct, those words are removed from the board. Guess wrong and it counts as a mistake—players get up to four mistakes until the game ends.
