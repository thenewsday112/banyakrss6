'Sharkman' Charged After Dragging Tagged Great White Onto Cape Cod Beach (Video)

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

A fishing influencer known as the “Sharkman” is facing criminal charges after allegedly catching a protected great white shark off Cape Cod, dragging it onto the beach, and celebrating the encounter on camera.

Christopher Stefanou, 30, of Long Island, New York, was indicted by a Barnstable County grand jury and arraigned October 21. The allegations stem from a nighttime fishing expedition near Wellfleet’s Marconi Beach in October 2025.

Stefanou, who posts as “LI Sharkman,” has amassed millions of YouTube subscribers with videos documenting dramatic shark catches. According to the Provincetown Independent, he traveled to Cape Cod with his crew and a customer from Amsterdam for what they called “Operation Amsterdam.”

Prosecutors allege the group used drones to drop baited lines offshore, hooked a great white, and fought it for more than an hour before hauling the exhausted animal onto the sand. They photographed the shark before removing the hook and releasing it.

The catch was no mystery to scientists. The shark, known as “AAron,” had been tagged in 2024 and tracked by researchers with the Massachusetts Division of Marine Fisheries and Atlantic White Shark Conservancy. Although prosecutors initially feared it had died from capture stress, its tracking tag was subsequently detected elsewhere.

Stefanou faces five charges: animal cruelty, illegal shore-based shark fishing, unlawful mechanized deployment of bait, attempting to capture a white shark, and possession of a prohibited species. The animal-cruelty charge alone carries a potential seven-year prison sentence, according to the Independent.

View this post on Instagram

Massachusetts prohibits targeting white sharks and using certain gear to lure large sharks toward shore in most Cape Cod waters. Regulations also restrict drone-deployed bait, partly to protect swimmers and surfers.

Ironically, investigators say Stefanou’s own social-media footage alerted them to the incident.

Stefanou pleaded not guilty.

His attorney, Jon Ciraulo, said, “He maintains his innocence and intends to fully challenge the allegations.”

Released on $10,000 bail, Stefanou must surrender his fishing license, remove related videos, and avoid fishing in Massachusetts waters. His next court date is December 11.

Lisandro Martinez is not a suitable player for Manchester United, says Peter Schmeichel (Reuters)

Peter Schmeichel branded Lisandro Martinez as ‘disgraceful’ following Manchester United’s 2-0 defeat to Chelsea on Saturday.

After a goalless first half, Morgan Rogers capitalised on a woeful display of defending from Martinez to put Chelsea in front in the 50th minute.

Xabi Alonso’s side then doubled their lead in the 64th minute through Cole Palmer’s spot-kick after Martinez was sent off for hitting Wesley Fofana with his forearm in United’s penalty area.

The defeat piled more pressure on Michael Carrick, who are now without a win in nine games, but Schmeichel believes the defeat to Chelsea is solely down to Martinez’s performance in the second half.

Children know the deepest truths about life, the elemental knowledge we unlearn in the act of living, those inarticulable truths music alone helps us remember. To hear children sing the secret wisdom of the heart is to be in the presence of something truer and more transcendent than any of our poems and postulates can touch — the ultimate affirmation of our aliveness, emanating Richard Powers’s beautiful and bittersweet assertion that “the use of music is to remind us how short a time we have a body.”

To celebrate twenty years of The Marginalian, composer extraordinaire Paola Prestini set six of her favorite bird divinations to music, which the Young People’s Chorus of New York City brought to life with their commanding innocence and their tender power. Paola’s perfect title for the piece, drawn from one of the cards, captures what every child is when this world is worth of its children: “Meteor of Joy.”

Couple with another choir of young people accompanying David Byrne’s anthem of faith in life, “One Fine Day,” then revisit the story and process behind these bird divinations.

The vintage bookstores of Mexico City's Roma neighbourhood influenced the interiors of this basement bar, designed by local outfit Em-Estudio.

Located below an early 20th-century house in the Mexican capital's historic colonia, Libreria Valladolid was designed to evoke the experience of exploring old bookshops.

"It captures the thrill felt when entering these places, searching for a unique story that has patiently waited among thousands of books on all topics and from many eras," said Em-Estudio.

Accessed from the street through an unassuming door, the entry vestibule features shelves packed with old volumes and more stacks along a wooden staircase that descends to another threshold.

Photo by Rene Villaseñor

Small lamps are squeezed between the spines and placed on top of the stacks, creating a moody glow that sets the scene for the bar beyond.

"The old bookstore is encountered upon entering, as you descend the stairs, and it remains behind as you cross the first threshold, where the books transform into the skin that embraces the rooms," said the studio.

Inside the basement, dark herringbone flooring and surfaces drenched in burgundy continue the bookshop atmosphere.

The larger walls are lined entirely with shelves filled with books stacked spine inwards, so the warm beige of their pages faces the room.

Cove lighting illuminates the books, creating a dramatic contrast with the otherwise shadowy bar, while banquettes below are accompanied by circular marble tables for placing drinks.

"The pages of thousands of books resting on bookshelves that reach the ceiling shed their literal meaning, becoming a tapestry in shades of white that only time can give, bathed in a gradient of warm light," Em-Estudio said.

Other areas such as the DJ booth, the bar and the bathrooms are highlighted with gold leaf and brass to draw the eye.

A further hidden lounge space is disguised by another bookshelf, decorated with cans and objects that "evoke a grocery-store bar".

This room swaps burgundy for green, applied in gloss across the ceiling and appearing in a long marble table that runs through the centre.

Dark wood Van Beuren chairs with yellow upholstery are lined up along each side, parallel to more walls of books that are uplit along each shelf.

A mirror that covers the back wall visually doubles the space, extending the table and the bookshelves in the reflection.

Em-Estudio was co-founded by architect Ivan Esqueda and interior designer Gala Sánchez-Renero – a husband and wife who also operate individual practices.

Their previous collaborations include a holiday home on a steep hillside in Oaxaca, Mexico, built from coloured concrete chosen to blend into the rocky mountainside.
