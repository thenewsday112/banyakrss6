Dezeen's top five houses of October 2026

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Featuring an architect's pink PoMo dreamhouse and a home for kids that looks like a wooden wardrobe, here are Dezeen's houses of the month for October.

Our monthly roundup of the most popular residences featured on Dezeen captures everything from a bungalow conversion in northern England to super-luxe villas in Ibiza and Switzerland.

Read on to find out more about Dezeen readers' favourite houses this month:

Photo by Juliet Murphy

Cloud House, UK, by Peter Morris

Architect Peter Morris wanted to give his “love of postmodernism a chance to reveal itself fully” with this pair of residences in north London, one inhabited by himself and his wife and another by a neighbour with similar tastes.

“The key goal was to make a beautiful building which I hoped might create a little moment of joy for anyone walking past it on the street,” Morris told Dezeen.

Find out more about this house ›

Photo by Aleš Jungmann

Wardrobe House, Czech Republic, by Šépka Architekti

As its name suggests, this house was intended to resemble a wooden wardrobe. Like a piece of flat-pack furniture, it was prefabricated as a kit of parts and assembled on-site.

Šépka Architekti designed the house for a couple who wanted to provide two of their teenage children with independent living space, and it sits in the garden of the main family home.

Find out more about this house ›

Photo by Jesus Orrico (also top)

Bellavista 11, Spain, by Fran Silvestre Arquitectos

This luxurious villa steps down a hillside on the eastern coast of Ibiza in three low-slung volumes.

Designed by Valencian studio Fran Silvestre Arquitectos, it is unmistakably minimalist, made up of pared-back white concrete rectangles.

Find out more about this house ›

Photos by Nate Cook

House on a Slope, Switzerland, by Think Architecture

An underground corridor (pictured left) connects the two parts of this house overlooking Lake Zurich, designed by Swiss studio Think Architecture.

The architects had been tasked with fitting a very large amount of floorspace onto a narrow, sloping site while being as sensitive as possible to the landscape.

Find out more about this house ›

Photo by Jake Balston

Deyne Avenue extension, UK, by Loader Monteith

A two-bed bungalow in Prestwich has been transformed into a four-bedroom family home with only a modest expansion of its footprint by Scottish architecture practice Loader Monteith.

In what the studio described as “squeezing a quart into a pint pot”, a rear extension and dormer loft conversion were added, along with a reorganisation of the floor plan.

On a fair afternoon in Queens, New York, a woman in a red dress crosses the street in front of me with an actual axe under her arm. Her face is stern, her step fast, and you can tell from the blood on her axe that she wasn’t using it to chop wood. A police siren wails in the distance, but that doesn’t seem to bother her. 

I reach to lock the car and then remember it’s Halloween. 

At a warm and lovely dinner at Cabinet Magazine, founder and editor-in-chief Sina Najafi shows us a photograph of tightrope walker Margret Zimmermann balancing on a high wire above the bombed-out ruins of the Haymarket in Cologne, Germany, in 1946. She looks her finest in her red lipstick, but a treacherous wind is blowing in her hair as a crowd watches from far below.  

Outside, Brooklyn seems to be getting richer and poorer at the same time. A man wearing a bejeweled crown asks me for money. In this city, performance artists are beggars and beggars are performance artists. 

At Hyperallergic’s art-themed Halloween party, dozens of guests show up with impressive costumes and really commit to the act. Claire Valdez, an artist and organizer who’s running for Congress, drops by and serves as a judge in our art costume contest. I don’t have a costume, but I perform all night. 

“To live is to believe,” Marcel Duchamp says in a video on my phone screen. A little girl dressed as Lady Liberty crosses the street with her mother, a torch in her hand. She keeps the torch held high as they walk off, and that makes me smile.

—Hakim Bishara, editor-in-chief

Garner On Everton Goal Threat & 'Real Chance' Cup Verdict

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

James Garner believes Everton have a “real chance” of making their mark in the Carabao Cup this season after Thursday night’s excellent 3-1 victory over Newcastle United. 

Garner was on the scoresheet at Hill Dickinson Stadium, chipping in for his first goal of the season.

With the Blues facing League Two’s Fleetwood Town in the quarter-finals of the competition in the next round, he believes the Blues should hold genuine belief they can go all the way in the competition.

“It was important for us to get the win and to go into the next round,” Garner told evertontv. “I think if we would have heard at the start of the competition that we’d be in the quarter-finals and facing Fleetwood, we would have taken it.

"So, it's a real chance for us to actually do something with this cup.

“It would be amazing for us as players, as a team, as a club, and for the fans as well, to hopefully go and bring some silverware back here.”

Among the goalscorers on Thursday, Garner hopes to continue building confidence and fitness after overcoming a groin injury at the start of the campaign.  

“It’s always a good feeling when you score a goal,” he added. “Unfortunately, I don't score enough, which I need to start doing, but to score a goal and to put the team on the front foot is a good feeling.  

“Good performances and even something small – like a goal – does boost your confidence. I just need to stay positive. 

“I know I came back really early with my injury – probably a bit too early – but that just shows the type of person I am.  

“I want to be there for the lads, and that's what I have done. 

“I'm pushing every single day to be 100 per cent, and I hope everyone can see that.”
