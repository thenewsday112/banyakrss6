How House Democrats' 'deterrence message' on Trump jobs could backfire

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Sen. Elissa Slotkin's warning to young Americans not to join the Trump administration could backfire by handing Trump officials legal ammunition to derail the investigations Democrats hope to launch if they retake Congress, a former federal prosecutor warned.

The freshman Michigan Democrat said last month that she wants her party to pursue "signature cases" against officials who helped Trump, reasoning that it would discourage young Americans from considering jobs in his administration.

"I want that deterrence message sent," Slotkin said during an Oct. 13 event at the University of Chicago, describing how young graduates might fear "buying myself a ton of problems" by joining the administration.

But Abhishek Kambli, a former deputy associate attorney general in Trump's Justice Department, said Slotkin's remarks could backfire if Democrats retake either chamber of Congress in the midterm elections and gain greater control over congressional investigations.

"If they just throw in boilerplate language that this is for legislative purpose, anyone challenging those subpoenas will have that entire record to demonstrate that it's anything but that," Kambli, a partner at Holtzman Vogel, told Fox News Digital.

HOUSE DEMS PLOT TRUMP INVESTIGATION BLITZ IF THEY SEIZE MAJORITY

While Congress has broad authority to conduct investigations, subpoena witnesses and review government operations, the Supreme Court has ruled that lawmakers cannot use those powers solely to punish individuals.

The Supreme Court confronted those limits in 2020, when House Democrats attempted to obtain Trump's personal financial records through congressional subpoenas.

In Trump v. Mazars, the justices established a four-factor test requiring courts to examine whether Congress demonstrated a legitimate legislative need for the records, whether its requests were sufficiently narrow, whether the information could be obtained elsewhere and what burden the subpoenas would impose on the president.

The court sent the dispute back to lower courts without ruling that the subpoenas satisfied those standards, leaving unresolved questions about how the framework would be applied.

"So it's not enough just to tack on a legislative purpose," Kambli said, arguing that courts must look beyond Congress' stated reasons for an investigation to determine whether its demands are constitutionally justified.

Democrats' public remarks could become evidence against them if future subpoenas are challenged, according to Kambli.

"They don't have to bury their head in the sand," Kambli said of the courts. "I think they have to look at the facts of the case and look at it in terms of how they fits into the broader comments that they made."

House Democrats are already laying the groundwork for investigations into Trump's family business dealings and potential conflicts of interest if they regain control of Congress. Sen. Richard Blumenthal, D-Conn., has also vowed to expand his investigation into donors funding Trump's White House ballroom if Democrats retake the Senate,

But another constitutional law expert questioned whether Democrats' remarks would carry enough legal weight to undermine future congressional investigations.

Michael Morley, a professor at Florida State University College of Law, said courts generally recognize that politicians bring partisan motivations to congressional investigations and would be reluctant to invalidate subpoenas merely because of provocative public remarks.

"I don't think that the fact that there might be partisan motivations for a congressional investigation would ultimately be a valid basis for refusing to comply with a subpoena," Morley told Fox News Digital.

"At the end of the day, a court is going to be very reluctant to conclude, based on one or more members' possible subjective motivations, that Congress lacks constitutional authority to investigate," Morley said.

He also emphasized that Trump v. Mazars focused specifically on subpoenas for the president's personal records, arguing that applying the same heightened legal standards to subpoenas targeting other administration officials would be a stretch.

But Morley said Slotkin's language could give judges pause when the legal basis for a subpoena is already uncertain.

"It's bad atmospherics," he said, adding that such statements could make courts "marginally less likely" to resolve doubts in Democrats' favor.

Slotkin has rejected accusations that she wants to target Americans merely for working for Republicans. She told CNN that she was talking about officials who break the law or misuse taxpayer dollars, calling the criticism "manufactured outrage."

But Kambli warned the consequences could extend beyond whether Democratic subpoenas survive legal challenges, saying that investigations could inflict financial and reputational damage even without criminal charges.

"There is the idea that the process is the punishment, and there is no doubt that some members of Congress intend to use the process to bankrupt people that can't afford legal representation," Kambli said.

He also said that while Trump's Justice Department would be unlikely to prosecute administration officials over criminal contempt referrals from a Democratic-controlled Congress, that could change under a future Democratic administration.

Morley also criticized the prospect of intimidating young government employees who may lack the resources to defend themselves.

"That's just not how we want our political disputes to be handled," Morley said.

XRP Reserves on Binance Slump to 2022 Lows — Is a Supply-Shock Rocket Surge Imminent?

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Could Ripple-linked XRP be sitting on the brink of a massive supply squeeze? Fresh on-chain data has revealed a striking development: XRP reserves on Binance have tumbled to their lowest level in nearly four years, with millions of tokens disappearing from the exchange’s holdings. The sudden drop is fueling speculation that a supply shock could be brewing, potentially setting the stage for an explosive price rally if investor demand returns.

XRP Vanishes From Binance as Exchange Reserves Hit 4-Year Lows 

While the broader crypto market continues to battle persistent selling pressure and weeks of volatility, XRP is flashing a potentially bullish on-chain signal. Despite struggling to gain upward momentum amid the downturn, the token is seeing its exchange reserves shrink, suggesting that an increasing amount of XRP is moving off trading platforms.

According to data shared by CryptoQuant analyst Amr Taha, XRP reserves on Binance plunged to 2.47 billion tokens on October 30, marking their lowest level since December 2022. 

Specifically, between October 28 and October 30, Binance’s XRP reserves fell from 2.69 billion to 2.47 billion tokens, representing a decrease of 220 million XRP, or approximately 8.18%, in just two days. The rapid drawdown has drawn attention to the remaining XRP on exchanges, raising questions about whether the trend could contribute to a potential supply squeeze if buying demand strengthens.

Per Taha, the decline in XRP reserves extended beyond Binance to other major exchanges. On Bybit, holdings fell from 306 million XRP to 286 million, a decrease of 20 million tokens. Meanwhile, Bithumb’s reserves slipped from 1.847 billion XRP to 1.837 billion XRP, marking a reduction of 10 million tokens.

Could XRP’s Supply Crunch Supercharge Its Price?

The decline in XRP reserves across multiple exchanges could reduce the supply readily available for immediate trading, potentially supporting a price recovery if buying demand strengthens. However, outflows alone do not confirm accumulation, as investors may simply be moving tokens to private wallets or other platforms.

For XRP bulls, the key question is whether declining exchange balances will coincide with a meaningful recovery in buying demand. If renewed investor interest meets a tighter supply of tokens available for sale, the combination could provide support for a strong price rally. Until then, the falling reserves remain an encouraging on-chain development for holders as XRP hovers around $1.25.

Two Honda Prelude Builds Show What a Type R Might Have Been

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

The Type R That Never Was

Some enthusiasts were not impressed with the Honda Prelude because its sporty styling and historic nameplate raised performance expectations, but its hybrid powertrain doesn’t necessarily meet them. The Prelude is powered by a 2.0-liter naturally aspirated inline-four with a two-motor hybrid system, delivering a combined output of 200 horsepower, slightly less than the CR-V Hybrid on paper.

However, enthusiasts may get what they want at the 2026 SEMA Show, where at least two custom Preludes will be showcased. Both feature Civic Type R powertrains, including a 2.0-liter turbocharged K20C1 engine that produces 315 horsepower in factory form and a 6-speed manual transmission. One was built by Hasport, a manufacturer of Honda and Acura engine-swap components, and will be displayed at the Honda Racing Corporation booth. The other comes from aftermarket tuning company throtl.

Honda

A Double Dose of Type R

The Hasport Prelude Club Racer is a track-focused build designed to compete in time attack events. Sporting a livery that pays tribute to the Hasport Prelude Circuit Monster, the custom car features a Borla exhaust system and suspension tuning derived from the Acura Integra Type S TCX race car.

On the other hand, throtl’s build is described as the “Prelude Type R that Honda never did.” This gives enthusiasts a reimagined version of what the Prelude could have looked like if it had come from the factory with a more potent powertrain. Well, Honda designed the production Prelude as a modern grand touring sports coupe that balances driving enjoyment, fuel efficiency, and everyday usability.

The aftermarket tuning company’s build is documented on its YouTube channel, which shows that the engine swap was far from straightforward, despite the Prelude and Civic sharing core underpinnings. There were several challenges, including modifying the front and rear bumpers and fabricating custom motor mounts.

Honda

Honda’s Racing Pedigree on Full Display

Aside from the Hasport Prelude Club Racer, the Honda Racing Corporation booth will showcase other performance vehicles at the upcoming SEMA Show, including the Civic Type R HRC Concept making its North American debut, 2027 Passport TrailSport Baja Chase, Honda-powered 2026 Aston Martin Aramco Formula 1 AMR26, and 2026 Indy 500-winning Honda IndyCar. HRC US will also introduce its first lineup of performance parts for both on-road and off-road vehicles.

The 2026 SEMA Show will run from November 3 to 6 at the Las Vegas Convention Center in Las Vegas, Nevada.

United Airlines’ JFK Return Is Imminent: What’s The Strategy, Though?

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

We know that United Airlines CEO Scott Kirby thinks that one of the carrier’s biggest mistakes in the past was pulling out of New York Kennedy Airport (JFK). He’s committed to changing that, and the airline still seems to think that it’s on track to return to the airport in 2027.

A lot has changed since this was initially a big topic of conversation, including United seemingly no longer being interested in any sort of merger with JetBlue. So with United potentially within a year of returning to JFK, I’d like to talk a bit more about what that could look like, as I’m still not sure I fully understand the strategy.

Why United left JFK, and how the airline plans to return

United obviously has a huge hub at Newark Liberty Airport (EWR), and different airline executives have different takes as to the overlap in demand between Newark and Kennedy.

Through late 2015, United had a relatively small presence at JFK, with a majority of the service consisting of premium transcontinental flights to Los Angeles (LAX) and San Francisco (SFO), with a product that was branded as p.s. The airline operated these flights with specially configured, three cabin Boeing 757-200s.

However, in late 2015, the airline made the decision to end service at JFK, and instead consolidate flights at EWR. The airline was reportedly losing money on these flights, and just couldn’t compete with American and Delta, which both had major hubs there.

Shortly after that decision was made, Kirby joined United, before eventually being appointed CEO. For as long as he has been at United, Kirby has felt that leaving JFK was one of the carrier’s biggest strategic blunders. In 2021, United briefly returned to JFK, operating transcon routes with Boeing 767-300ERs. However, the airline pulled out by 2022, as it couldn’t get permanent slots, and couldn’t scale service in a way that made sense.

United returned to JFK in 2021, but it didn’t last long

Ever since, United has been determined to return to JFK. We know that United wants up to 20 daily slot pairs at the airport, to offer very frequent coast-to-coast service. So, where do things stand? As flagged by JonNYC, United Chief Commercial Officer Andrew Nocella recently went on record to confirm that United is on track to return to JFK in 2027, which is just around the corner.

So, how will United secure the slots needed for service?

  • We know that JetBlue and United have their Blue Sky partnership, and that includes the possibility of JetBlue giving United up to seven daily slot pairs at JFK as early as 2027 (with “up to” and “as early as” being the key terms here)
  • United is reportedly exploring opportunities to acquire slot pairs from low margin carriers that aren’t doing well at JFK; however, those are unlikely to be very lucrative in terms of the timing of flights
United is planning to get some slot pairs from JetBlue

I still have a hard time envisioning United’s JFK return

United management is generally on top of things when it comes to strategy, and I can make sense of most things. However, I still struggle to fully rationalize the plans for returning to JFK. That’s not to say it doesn’t make sense, or that I’m right, but here’s how I think through this:

  • I struggle to see how JetBlue is getting sufficient upside from its partnership with United that it thinks it makes sense to give or trade seven slot pairs with United, just so that United can compete directly in two of JetBlue’s highest revenue markets
  • Even if United managed to secure 20 daily slot pairs for premium transcontinental routes, how will the airline do that without hemorrhaging money in the market? The airline is at a disadvantage in terms of customer loyalty at JFK, has more limited connecting traffic on the JFK side, etc. (though admittedly it’s better positioned on the West Coast)
  • I have to imagine that a major incentive with a return to JFK is being able to compete for Asia-bound traffic via the SFO hub, so the route could be used to fill connecting transpacific flights
  • How will United compete with the ground experience offered by competitors, when you have Delta offering a dedicated Delta One check-in facility, Delta One Lounge, etc.?

On the surface, entering a market as a fourth place player, with limited opportunity to grow, little connectivity on the JFK side, and limited ability to have a great ground experience, seems like it could create a challenge.

How is United supposed to compete with the Delta ground experience?

Bottom line

United Airlines executives still insist the airline will return to JFK in 2027. This seems to involve getting some number of slots from JetBlue (as part of their partnership), and then possibly acquiring slots from some other carriers. It’s still not entirely clear how this will play out, though last we heard, the goal is to get 20 slot pairs, and aggressively fly to LAX and SFO.

I know Kirby thinks it was a big mistake to leave JFK, though I remain a bit confused by how the airline offering high frequency service from JFK to the West Coast, when the markets are already served by three large carriers, will be lucrative.

How do you see United’s JFK strategy playing out?
