This top data scientist’s (non-AI) model finds Republicans have just one chance in six of holding Senate

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

I started following Thomas Miller’s political forecasts during the January 2021 Senate races in Georgia. Just a few days before the election, the polls and pundits anticipated a relatively easy win for both Republican candidates. But Miller—a prominent data scientist and professor at Northwestern University—posited a Democratic sweep, and he was right. He also called Trump’s victory in 2024. What’s different about Miller’s models? They’re far more current than the polls, and they’re centered on prediction market prices that are typically a better guide than the voter surveys—plus Miller adjusts the outlook in every Senatorial contest by using all we know about every contest.

Now, Miller is applying an updated version of his model for the current Senate elections. You can find the stats on his website, Virtualtout.io, and I highly recommend paying a visit. Once again, Miller starts by incorporating the betting venues’ data. “I use the numbers from two sites, Polymarket and Kalshi,” he told Fortune. “They’re the ones that trade the most dollars, where most of the action happens.” Miller then calibrates the posted “prices” so that they reflect probabilities, and the odds for both leading candidates tally to 100%. In a further step, he deploys multiple time series analysis to find underlying factors driving the betting prices, and uses trusted models from finance and economics to project how they’re likely to influence voters moving forward.

By the way, he thinks his model works better than AI.

Miller updates the probabilities of a Republican or Democratic win every 10 minutes. The odds that a candidate will win scroll across the top of the Virtualtout.io screen continuously, in a band resembling a stock ticker tape. For each race, Miller also posts the change over the last 24 hours. Miller calls the streaming feature the “NowCast.”

As for those “underlying” factors, one force swamps all others: Donald Trump. “The states don’t move independently of one another,” says Miller. “If one goes up for Democrats, chances are they’ll all go up for Democrats, and vice versa. The entire market is dominated by what Trump does and says hour by hour.” Put simply, when voters don’t like the POTUS’s words or actions, all Republican odds tend to fall. Miller asserts that his model has identified 85% to 90% of what’s causing the variances on Kalshi and Polymarket, and “my interpretation is that it’s overwhelmingly Trump and the Republican positions generally.”

Though he opened Virtuatout.io to public view about a week ago, Miller started running the numbers on September 17. What’s most striking is just how far the GOP chances have faded in just six weeks.

As of around 9 AM on October 30, five days before most polling sites close, Virtualtout.io gives the Republicans a 17.1%, or just over one-sixth probability, of winning 50 seats and hence retaining control of the upper chamber. On September 17, that figure was twice as high at 34.8%, or slightly better than one-in-three. In all five contests where the Democrats can flip a seat, their chances are better than 60%—in most cases, much better.

In North Carolina, where Republican Thom Tillis is retiring, leaving an open seat, the GOP was never remotely close, and now harbors a minuscule 4% shot at victory. In Texas, the Republican odds have dropped since September 17 from 46.15% to 31.9%, in Ohio from 45.4% to 31.8%, and in Alaska from 32.95% to 28.6%.

In only one state is the GOP faring better than in mid-September and that’s Maine. Republican Susan Collins started as an underdog at 32.6%, but has clawed back to 39.9%, adding 1.7 points in the last 24 hours. In Alaska, things are also looking up for the Republicans: After falling to a low of 23.4% on October 23, incumbent Dan S. Sullivan’s prospects have improved by over 5 points to 28.6%.

For the political pros looking for harbingers of big trouble for either side, the towering sign is the Republican woes in traditional strongholds Iowa and Kansas. In the former, Republican Ashley Hinton dropped from 61.75% on September 17 to a virtual tie at 53.4%, a trend mirrored in Kansas where Roger Marshall was looking a likely victor six weeks ago at 75.8%; now, he’s down almost 19 points at 57.1%. In the last day, Hinton and Marshall have shed 1.45% and 2.47 points respectively.

In the one state where Republicans saw a big chance for a flip, the horizon’s receding fast. The GOP chances of beating Abdul El-Sayed in Michigan have slipped from better than a third in mid-September to about a quarter (25.4%).

If the Democrats indeed score a flip in every contest where their probabilities in Miller’s model stand at 60% or better, and hold all the seats they have now, they’ll clinch a majority of 52 to 48, nearly reversing the Republican dominance at 53-47.

In South Carolina, recently rerated on the Fox News Channel from “lean Republican” to “tossup,” Miller still has Darline Graham at a commanding 75%, though she’s slipped 2.4 points in the past 24 hours.

Keep in mind that Miller’s setting odds, not making definitive predictions. A candidate at over 30% still stands a decent chance of winning. And Republicans are over that bogey in Maine, Ohio, and Texas, and close and trending the right way in Alaska. Odds-defying events happen all the time. The problem for Republicans is that they’d have to beat those odds multiple times, plus defend seats they never dreamed they’d have to defend.

It’s still far from over. And one of the best places to get a plausible, non-AI driven snapshot from a data scientist who’s been doing this for six years, is that NowCast on Tom Miller’s Virtualtout.io.

The term cardio often elicits grumbles as people picture punishing treadmill sessions and trail runs. But add the word cozy before it, and cardio is suddenly…a lot more palatable. That’s the gist behind the “cozy cardio” trend, which took off on social media in 2024 and resurfaced in 2026. The concept is simple: perform low- to moderate-intensity workouts in a comfortable, inviting environment.

Cozy cardio could look like setting out a few candles in your living room, dimming the lights, and turning on your favorite Netflix show while you stroll on a walking pad. Or it could involve donning a comfy velvet sweatsuit and sipping a delicious beverage while going for an easy walk on your favorite trail or through your neighborhood.

Cozy cardio looks different for everyone, according to Hope Zuckerbrow, the TikToker credited with starting the trend. But no matter how you practice it, the overarching objective is the same: take the pressure off exercise and actually enjoy movement.

As a certified personal trainer, I wholeheartedly support that notion and think there are several key benefits that outdoor athletes could reap from embracing the trend.

Cozy Cardio Can Keep You Consistent When Life Gets in the Way–or Motivation Wanes

When it comes to exercise, many people have an all-or-nothing mindset—especially athletes who take their outdoor sport seriously. They have a set workout plan—for example, a five-mile run or an hour-long strength session—and when something thwarts it, they might throw in the towel and do nothing at all.

But staying consistent with your workout routine is key to reaping the mental and physical benefits of exercise. So a better approach is to still do some form of movement even if it looks different than what you’d originally envisioned. That’s where cozy cardio can come in.

Because the trend is all about lowering the stakes on exercise, it can feel really doable, even on those days when life gets in the way, or you’re not in the mood to do anything.

Plus, cozy cardio creates an environment that makes working out a pleasurable experience. And that’s important, because actually enjoying exercise is associated with actually sticking with a workout routine, according to research.

Gentle Movement Can Promote Recovery

Another benefit of cozy cardio is that it can serve as active recovery in the wake of long, hard workouts. Research suggests doing even six to ten minutes of active recovery after a workout may boost overall performance.

So engaging in a short cozy cardio session after a big hike, ride, or run could inject a little more energy into your next outdoor adventure.

Steady-State Cardio Can Bolster Your Aerobic Fitness

A 2026 study found that low-intensity exercises improve both aerobic fitness and cardiometabolic health, which refers to factors involving the heart and metabolism, including blood pressure and cholesterol.

If you choose a steady-state form of exercise for your cozy cardio session, such as walking or a bike ride, and keep your effort low, you’ll likely tick the box for Zone 1 or Zone 2 cardio—both of which, when done consistently, can help build your aerobic fitness, or your VO2 max. VO2 max describes how efficiently your body takes in oxygen and transports it to the rest of the body so that your muscles can use it during physical activity. The higher your VO2, the better your aerobic fitness.

Overall, one of the biggest benefits of improving your aerobic health is going longer and having more energy during your favorite outdoor sport.

Tokenized securities are advancing into mainstream finance — but their expansion is exposing a problem that blockchain technology alone cannot solve: how to maintain a unified market when the same financial asset can exist across multiple trading platforms and digital networks at once.

Banks, exchanges, and asset managers are racing to build blockchain-based infrastructure to modernize securities trading. Yet the promised benefits — faster settlement, continuous trading — hinge on a harder question: can these systems preserve liquidity, legal ownership, and reliable price formation as they scale?

The stakes are coming into sharper focus as traditional institutions move beyond experimental pilots. Tokenization may reduce operational friction, but without coordinated market infrastructure, it risks introducing new inefficiencies into markets that already depend on an intricate web of relationships between brokers, custodians, and clearinghouses.

Tokenized Stocks Could Create Parallel Markets

A tokenized share doesn’t necessarily behave like an ordinary share traded through a regulated stock exchange.

Different tokenization arrangements can grant investors very different ownership rights, legal claims, and relationships with the underlying securities.

Some structures represent direct interests in the financial instrument itself; others amount to claims against an intermediary that holds the asset. Crucially, a blockchain record alone does not establish that an investor holds the same rights as a registered shareholder—those rights depend entirely on the instrument’s legal structure and the ownership arrangements behind it.

Fragmentation adds another layer of complication

If tokenized versions of a company’s shares trade across several platforms, liquidity could splinter among venues operating under different rules. For directories and market participants tracking the broader crypto-financial landscape—such as Cryptwerk—the proliferation of trading venues underscores how fragmented digital asset ecosystems can become. Investors could encounter inconsistent prices, varying settlement arrangements, and differences in how corporate actions are processed from one venue to the next.

These risks grow sharper when tokenized products are issued or traded independently of the companies whose shares they represent.

For exchanges, the commercial challenge is significant. A platform offering continuous trading still has to attract enough buyers and sellers to support efficient transactions. Extended hours deliver little value if investors face wider spreads or thin liquidity outside established market sessions.

Faster Settlement Requires More Than Blockchain

One of tokenization’s most compelling advantages is the ability to coordinate the transfer of a security and its payment within a single programmable system.

Traditional settlement involves multiple institutions verifying transactions, updating records, and arranging the movement of assets and funds. Tokenization could streamline parts of that process by allowing related actions to execute together.

The Bank for International Settlements has studied this possibility through research on unified ledgers that combine tokenized financial assets with regulated forms of money. Such arrangements could support delivery-versus-payment transactions — reducing the risk that one party hands over an asset without receiving the corresponding payment.

But settlement efficiency depends on far more than a blockchain network’s raw speed.

Financial institutions still have to determine which assets can be used for payment, when a transfer becomes legally final, and how each transaction interacts with existing clearing and custody arrangements. A tokenized security might transfer in seconds while the underlying cash movement or legal reconciliation still runs on conventional infrastructure.

That creates a critical gap between technical settlement speed and the completion of a legally enforceable transaction. For institutional investors, that second ultimately matters.

Europe’s Tokenization Experiments Reveal Practical Barriers

Europe offers a cautionary example of how regulatory permission doesn’t automatically produce an active market.

In June 2025, the European Securities and Markets Authority reported that the EU’s Distributed Ledger Technology Pilot Regime had attracted only three authorized market infrastructures — with minimal live trading activity. The regulator pointed to obstacles including interoperability, access to central bank money, and restrictions on the types and volumes of assets eligible platforms could handle, and recommended adjustments intended to encourage broader participation.

These findings matter well beyond Europe.

The growing involvement of banks, asset managers, and stock exchanges is reshaping the industry’s priorities. The Cryptocurrency Post has explored this expansion of tokenization beyond crypto-native companies, including the role traditional financial institutions are playing in building regulated digital asset infrastructure. Their participation makes system compatibility far more important.

A tokenized bond issued through one regulated platform may not be transferable to another without additional technical arrangements, legal agreements, or intermediaries. A financial institution might be unable to use a tokenized asset as collateral unless its counterparties and settlement providers recognize the instrument.

Interoperability, in other words, involves far more than connecting blockchains. It requires common standards for asset ownership, transfer restrictions, compliance, and settlement finality.

Institutional Adoption Will Depend on Market Quality

The success of tokenized securities is unlikely to depend on how many assets end up represented on blockchain networks.

Institutional investors evaluate financial products on liquidity, execution costs, counterparty exposure, and legal certainty. Tokenized products have to meet all of those requirements while demonstrating a clear operational advantage over the alternatives already in use.

Consider a tokenized government bond. It could prove genuinely useful in collateral management — but only if it can move efficiently between approved counterparties and settlement systems. Its value would come from cutting delays and operational costs, not from simply existing on a blockchain. The same logic applies to equities: a tokenized stock market has to offer dependable pricing, enforceable shareholder rights, and access to real liquidity.

Institutions also face a structural choice: build their own independent blockchain platforms, or participate in shared infrastructure. Separate systems may offer more control, but they can drive up integration costs and complicate transactions between institutions. Shared platforms could ease some of that friction — though only with agreement on governance, technical standards, and operational responsibilities.

Ultimately, tokenization may become a routine component of financial infrastructure without spawning a separate market for every digital representation of an asset. The strongest applications are likely to improve existing securities processes while preserving the protections and liquidity investors already expect.

For Wall Street, the central question is no longer whether financial assets can be tokenized. It’s whether the resulting markets can operate as reliably and efficiently as the systems they’re meant to improve.

What Is EIP-7702? Ethereum's Smart Account Feature Explained, and How to Stay Safe

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

On October 29, 2026, an Ethereum hot wallet belonging to Binance was emptied in a single transaction.

The tool behind the drain was EIP-7702, a feature that went live on Ethereum in May 2025.

As of October 30, 2026, no flaw in EIP-7702 itself has been reported.

It did exactly what a valid signature told it to do, and that is the part worth understanding.

Key Takeaways

●      EIP-7702 lets a regular Ethereum wallet run smart contract code, and it went live on May 7, 2025.

●      A token approval covers one token for one app, while an EIP-7702 delegation hands a contract your whole account.

●      Every delegation needs the wallet’s own signature, so the Binance hack looks like a signing-side failure, not an Ethereum bug.

●      Exchange users should watch official notices, withdrawal status, proof of reserves and the post-mortem.

●      Self-custody users only face EIP-7702 risk if they signed a delegation, which Etherscan’s Delegated Address field shows.

●      A delegation can be reset to the zero address, but if you don’t remember signing it, treat the wallet as compromised.

What Is EIP-7702?

EIP-7702 is an Ethereum upgrade that lets a regular crypto wallet, known as an externally owned account or EOA, borrow smart contract code and behave like a smart account.

It shipped with the Pectra upgrade, which went live on Ethereum mainnet on May 7, 2025.

The official specification, EIP-7702: Set Code for EOAs, adds a new transaction type that carries signed “authorizations.”

Each one names a contract and must be signed with the wallet’s own private key.

Our overview of the Ethereum Pectra upgrade covers the rest of that release.

How Does EIP-7702 Work?

It starts with a signature.

You sign an authorization that names a contract, the chain it covers and your account’s nonce, which is simply a running count of your transactions.

Any account can then submit that authorization to Ethereum, which is how a third party can pay the fee for you.

Once it lands, the network writes a small pointer on your account, which the specification calls a delegation indicator.

From then on, calls to your address run the code of the contract it points to, while your private key keeps full control of the account.

To undo it, you sign a new authorization that points to the zero address, and the account goes back to being a plain wallet.

How Does EIP-7702 Bring Account Abstraction to Wallets?

Account abstraction is the push to make Ethereum wallets programmable instead of limited to one signature and one action at a time.

EIP-7702 brings that idea to existing wallets, so nobody has to move funds to a new address.

The specification names three uses:

●      Batching: several actions, such as approve and swap, in one all-or-nothing transaction

●      Sponsorship: another account pays the network fee for you and can be repaid in a token other than ETH

●      Privilege de-escalation: a sub-key with narrow permissions, such as spending tokens but not ETH

Each of these depends on the contract your wallet points to, which is why the next section matters.

How Is an EIP-7702 Delegation Different From a Token Approval?

A token approval lets one app move one token, usually up to a set amount.

An EIP-7702 delegation hands the logic of your entire account to a contract, covering every asset inside it, until you reset it.

That gap in scope is why a bad delegation does far more damage than a bad approval.

Think of your wallet as a house.

A token approval is like handing someone the key to one cabinet to take out one item.

An EIP-7702 delegation is closer to hiring a house manager who runs every room.

A good manager, like a reputable wallet app’s smart account contract, only follows your instructions.

A malicious one can clear out the whole house in one trip.

** **

Your private key stays the master key, which also means anyone holding it can hire a new manager.

What Happened in the Binance EIP-7702 Hack?

On October 29, 2026, an attacker used signed EIP-7702 authorizations to point Binance’s Ethereum hot wallet at the attacker’s own contract.

Minutes later, one transaction emptied it.

Early on-chain estimates put losses across Binance’s wallets in the billions of dollars as of October 30, 2026.

Public on-chain data shows four steps:

●      Sign: Around 20:20 UTC, a batch of authorizations signed with the wallet’s key landed on Ethereum.

●      Point: The wallet’s nonce kept changing, so the batch reportedly covered many values, and the one that matched handed the wallet to the attacker’s contract.

●      Sweep: About three minutes later, one call moved out ETH, stablecoins and dozens of other tokens.

●      Reset:Almost four hours later, the delegation was revoked.
Step one is the key detail: a delegation needs the wallet’s own valid signature, so either the key itself leaked or a system that holds it signed when it should not have.

Was It a Leaked Private Key or a Tricked Signing System?

As of October 30, 2026, Binance had not published a root cause.

Two explanations are on the table, pointing to very different weak spots.

The first is a leaked private key, a familiar failure with a familiar fix: retire the key and lock down everyone who had access.

The second is more unsettling.

Many exchanges route hot wallet signing through automated services that check where funds are going and how much is moving.

An EIP-7702 authorization is not a normal transfer.

It is a short signed message naming a contract, with no recipient or amount for those rules to inspect.

If a signing service approved one without decoding it, the exchange’s own security layer signed away control of the wallet.
Early reports also described funds leaving on blockchains that have no EIP-7702 feature at all, which, if confirmed, would point to the signing side.

Is My Money Still Safe on an Exchange After the Binance Hack?

A hot wallet is only the slice of an exchange’s funds kept online for withdrawals.

Whether customers are made whole depends on the exchange’s reserves and what it formally commits to, such as a protection fund, so trust official updates over social media posts.

Watch for:

●      Announcements on Binance’s own website and verified accounts

●      Deposit and withdrawal status for each network

●      An updated proof of reserves report

●      A post-mortem that names the root cause and the fix

Scammers move fast after big hacks, so check any “refund” link against our guide to spotting crypto scams first.

Proof of reserves is an exchange’s published evidence that its holdings cover what it owes users, and our guide on how to assess whether an exchange’s funds are safe explains how to read one.

On MEXC, users can confirm their own balance is included in the reserve data through a Merkle tree check.

Should I Move My Crypto Off an Exchange After a Hack?

A hack at one exchange does not automatically put your funds at risk, and moving them is a personal call.

If you do move some, send a small test amount first and double-check the network and address before the full transfer.

For how often breached exchanges have repaid users, see our data on past exchange hacks.

To see your options, our guide to Binance alternatives compares eight exchanges by region and fees.

A direct MEXC vs Binance comparison sets fees, listings and security claims side by side.

If you decide to switch, the walkthrough on how to move crypto from Binance to MEXC covers network choice and test transfers.

Does EIP-7702 Put My Own Wallet at Risk?

Not unless you signed a delegation.

Holding ETH in a self-custody wallet does not switch EIP-7702 on, and there is nothing to “upgrade.”

Ethereum’s official site says anyone telling you to “upgrade” your ETH is trying to scam you.

The real risk is phishing.

A fake airdrop or website can ask you to sign an authorization that points your wallet at a malicious smart contract, disguised as a routine prompt.

Etherscan’s guide to EIP-7702 smart accounts, published in June 2025, said over 97% of all delegations were linked to scams targeting vulnerable users.

One more catch: an authorization signed with a chain ID of zero is valid on every EVM chain that supports EIP-7702, as the EIP-7702 guidelines on ethereum.org warn.

How Do I Check and Revoke an EIP-7702 Delegation?

Paste your address into Etherscan and find the Delegated Address field.

If block explorers are new to you, see our guide to how an Ethereum blockchain explorer works.

“Null” means no delegation.

If it shows a contract that isn’t your wallet app’s, reset it from inside the wallet.

●      Step 1: A known wallet contract, such as MetaMask’s EIP-7702 Delegator, simply means you turned on that wallet’s smart account.

●      Step 2: If you don’t recognize the contract, revoke it in your wallet app, which delegates to the zero address.

●      Step 3: Repeat the check on other EVM chains where you use the same address.

●      Step 4: If you don’t remember signing that delegation, assume your key or seed phraseis exposed and move everything to a new wallet.
Revoking won’t rescue a wallet whose key has leaked, because whoever holds the key can sign a fresh delegation a minute later.

Could an EIP-7702 Attack Happen Again?

EIP-7702 is a finalized, live part of Ethereum, so the feature is not going away.

The clearest fix sits with whoever controls signing: systems that decode every authorization and refuse any contract that isn’t pre-approved.

The specification itself says there is no safe way to let apps request delegations freely.

FAQ

Is EIP-7702 a bug in Ethereum?

No, it is an intended feature with Final status, and every delegation needs the wallet’s own signature.

What is the difference between EIP-7702 and ERC-4337?

Both move Ethereum toward account abstraction, but EIP-7702 upgrades an existing wallet while ERC-4337 relies on separate smart contract accounts.

Which Ethereum upgrade added EIP-7702?

EIP-7702 shipped in the Pectra upgrade, which activated on May 7, 2025.

How do I revoke an EIP-7702 delegation?

Sign a new delegation to the zero address from your wallet app, which resets the account to a plain wallet.

Does an EIP-7702 delegation work on other chains?

Only on the chain it names, unless it was signed with chain ID 0, which makes it valid on every EVM chain that supports it.

Was EIP-7702 hacked in the Binance incident?

As of October 30, 2026, no flaw in EIP-7702 had been reported, and the delegation carried the wallet’s own valid signature.

Do I need to do anything with my ETH because of EIP-7702?

No, your ETH needs no upgrade, and you only need to act if you find a delegation you don’t recognize.

Is my money on Binance safe after the EIP-7702 hack?

As of October 30, 2026, Binance had not published a root cause, so rely on its official announcements and proof of reserves rather than social media posts.

The Bottom Line on EIP-7702

EIP-7702 gives ordinary Ethereum wallets real smart account powers, and it also lets one signature hand over an entire account.

The Binance hack showed that power at full scale, with no Ethereum bug reported as of October 30, 2026.
