Computer Simulations Suggest Building Blocks of Life Didn't Arrive on Prehistoric Comets

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →
CNN reports: The popular theory that comets were crucial in kick-starting life on Earth billions of years ago may not hold up, according to new computer simulations that counter decades of scientific thinking. The finding suggests the fragile organic molecules supposedly carried by the icy objects would have been destroyed on impact, upending an established origin of life theory that dates back to at least 1961 [with earlier versions postulated in 1908]... Early in its history — over 4 billion years ago — Earth may have experienced a period of heavy bombardment from more than 100,000 comets larger than 100 meters (328 feet), according to the new study, published Wednesday in the journal Scientific Reports. Those impacts, however, were mostly too heavy for life-starting molecules to withstand. The team used the latest models of comet formation and evolution to simulate this early planetary period 100,000 times. "Given what we know about surface environments on the early Earth, on average, our simulations found that there were no successful impacts," said lead study author Richard Anslow, who conducted the research while completing his doctorate at the Institute of Astronomy of the University of Cambridge in the United Kingdom... "The typical velocities of these comets when they impact the Earth meant that actually very few, if any, would have been able to deliver intact organic molecules, so comets actually don't seem to be as useful for prebiotic chemistry as was previously thought," Anslow said. More precisely, 54% of the simulations showed no impact at all, while the remaining 46% had at least one and a "tiny minority" returned up to three collisions, Anslow said. "We can't rule out confidently that there was never a successful impact — there could have been one of these fluke impacts." However, such flukes had to occur in a "perfect location" that already met several important conditions, including the presence of liquid water and other chemicals needed to support life. That combination makes the overall process unlikely, he added. The article notes his argument that even if a successful impact occurred, it's unlikely an important chemical like hydrogen cyanide (HCN) could survive the impact, and would've dissolved in an ocean impact. Plus, "many other precursor molecules are also needed, such as compounds that contain phosphorus and sulfur, but these are not routinely found in comets, Anslow said." An alternative cosmic source of organic material could have been asteroids, which typically struck Earth more slowly and in greater numbers. Anslow pointed to recent findings on samples from asteroids Bennu and Ryugu, which contain the majority of protein-building amino acids. The recent study also raises another intriguing possibility: While Earth may not have had the ideal conditions to foster HCN from comet impacts, ancient Mars might have been better suited. "Because Mars is much smaller than Earth and further away from the sun, the typical impact velocity of comets is much lower, so while these impacts on Earth were destroying most of the HCN, this wasn't the case so much for Mars," Anslow said. This idea could add another dimension to the long-standing question of whether the red planet once supported life. While the authors closely analyzed how HCN could have survived comet impact, the chemical may have undergone reactions before the icy bodies entered Earth's atmosphere, said Queenie Chan, an associate professor of Earth sciences at Royal Holloway University of London, who was not involved with the study. This process could have created biologically important compounds, such as those protein building blocks, that are less volatile and more likely to survive.

By: Oliver Fisher

AC Milan recorded a huge win in the derby against Inter, scoring three times in the first half to secure a 3-1 win.

It did not take long for Milan to go in front, with Christian Pulisic heading in a cross from Diego Moreira. Then, just a few minutes later, Adrien Rabiot was on hand to fire in a corner first time and make it a two-goal lead.

Alessandro Bastoni scored from a corner to make it a one-goal lead once again, but then Moreira’s free-kick re-established the two-goal lead and that would be enough to see the Rossoneri through to the end of the game.

It marked a fourth straight win for Milan in the derby and it reduces the gap to the city rivals back down to four points.

First half: A red-hot start

Ruben Amorim chose to make six changes from the team that lost to Bologna on Wednesday night, with Mario Gila, Sankhoun Diawara, Luka Modric, Adrien Rabiot, Diego Moreira and Christian Pulisic all getting the nod.

Inside four minutes, Milan took the lead and it was the derby talisman Christian Pulisic who struck once again. It was quite a simple move down the right side in the end, with Diego Moreira checking back inside onto his left foot and whipped in a simply perfect cross for Pulisic to nod in from a few yards out.

Then, just a few minutes later, Milan doubled their advantage from a set-piece after another good spell of pressure. A low delivery from Moreira found the run of Rabiot towards the near post, who somehow managed to divert it in via the far post to make it 2-0.

Midway through the first half, Inter got themselves back into the game through a dead ball of their own. A corner was flicked on at the near post towards Bastoni, who was on hand to nod in from a few yards with the Milan defenders rooted.

Inter assumed the ascendancy after the goal, and they twice threatened in quick succession shortly after their first goal. Calhanoglu had a shot tipped over from the edge of the box, then another flicked header from a corner drew a desperate reaction save from Maignan.

Photo by Marco Luzzani/Getty Images

Milan continued to give their city rivals opportunities to try and make it a brand new game. A silly foul by Pavlovic on Spence drew a free-kick just off the right edge of the box, though the eventual shot from Diouf was grasped by Maignan.

Just over 10 minutes before the break, Inter had another chance to draw themselves level. A cross from the left was not cut out by Koni De Winter, and the ball fells to Spence who fired with his left foot, but the Frenchman makes a brilliant reaction save to deflect the shot out for a corner.

Just two minutes before the break, Milan restored their two-goal advantage thanks to a quite simply beautiful free-kick from Diego Moreira. After getting two assists to his name already on the night, he turned scorer with a fantastic attempt that was up and over the wall, dipping just enough to land inside the near right post.

Second half: Guiding it home

Ten minutes into the second half, Inter had the first big opening and it fell to their recent danger man Carlos Augusto. A cross to the far post wasn’t tracked by anyone and the wing-back ghosted in at the far post, heading it wide from about a yard off the ground.

With 25 minutes left, a corner this time for Milan: Modric’s cross from the left finds Rabiot at the near post, who tries to head it in, but his connection was poor and the ball went straight out.

In the 75th minute, it looked like the game was going to be on once again when the referee gave a penalty for what he adjudged to be a handball on Mario Gila. The replays show that the cross struck his midriff, and therefore the penalty was overturned to be a corner.

With the game settled into a rhythm of attack versus defence, Amorim chose to make a double change with eight minutes to go. Pulisic and Diawara were the ones who came off, with Alphadjo Cisse and Davide Bartesaghi the ones to come on.

In the penultimate minute of added time, a double sub came from Amorim. Ramos and Moreira came off, Filippo Terracciano and Omari Hutchinson came on.

Alphadjo Cisse came on inside the final minutes and immediately came close to scoring. The Italian forward tried a curling shot from outside the area, but Martinez saved well to put it out for a corner, keeping it 3-1.

Is This the Beginning of the End for Nuno? West Ham Fans Are Losing Patience

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Perhaps it’s not the ideal time to write in the immediate aftermath of a 3-1 home defeat. Yet after back-to-back losses and six goals conceded across those matches, it would be equally wrong to ignore the growing concerns.

The reality is stark. While other clubs around us are building momentum, we’ve slipped from third to eighth in the table. That’s not a minor setback, it’s a worrying trend.

The statistics only reinforce what supporters are witnessing on the pitch.

At half-time, we had managed just one shot on target, producing an xG of 0.58 compared to West Brom’s 1.39. To be fair, West Brom had only registered one shot on target themselves. However, after the break our xG improved to 0.98 thanks to four additional efforts on target. West Brom, meanwhile, generated an xG of 2.06 and demonstrated the clinical edge that we’re currently lacking, scoring three times from just three shots on target.

The most pressing concern is undoubtedly the defence. Hermansen looks completely devoid of confidence, and calls for Areola’s return are becoming louder with every passing week. Meanwhile, Morato’s deployment on the left side increasingly resembles a failed experiment. I had reservations when the signing was made and wrote about those concerns at the time. Unfortunately, those fears now appear to be justified, and there’s no satisfaction in being proven right.

But the issues extend far beyond individual players.

This squad is underperforming, and ultimately responsibility lies with the manager. Nuno is now facing genuine pressure. Automatic promotion was the objective at the start of the season. The uncertainty of the play-offs, or being dragged into the lower half of the table was never part of the plan. Yet the uncomfortable truth is that we are becoming far too easy to play against. Opponents understand our weaknesses and are exploiting them week after week.

If that sounds overly critical, so be it.

Looking back at the season so far, the only performances I can confidently say I thoroughly enjoyed were the victories against Wrexham and perhaps Wolves. The 1-0 away win at Swansea also deserves recognition, and I guess the strong win against Lincolns nine man team. Beyond that, supporters have largely witnessed inconsistent displays, and the current criticism is a direct consequence of what they’ve seen on the pitch. With the quality available to Nuno, this team should be producing more.

Off the pitch, there are still unanswered questions.

I’ve consistently defended Kretinsky, yet we are still waiting for formal confirmation regarding the shareholder situation, while Sullivan remains in the directors’ box. The uncertainty surrounding the club continues to create an unwelcome backdrop.

So, is it too early to suggest that Nuno’s position should be under review?

Not anymore.

That conversation is beginning to gain real traction among supporters. It’s worth remembering that after relegation, Nuno’s departure appeared inevitable. It was only the intervention of Kretinsky that kept him at the club. Even Nuno himself seemed to believe his time was up.

Right now, the club still feels unsettled, both on and off the pitch.

The first step? A convincing win against Cardiff. Because if that doesn’t arrive, the pressure on everyone involved will only intensify.

Serie A | Milan 3-1 Inter: Diego Moreira inspiration for derby success

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Diego Moreira inspired Milan’s fourth consecutive Derby della Madonnina victory with a goal and assists for Christian Pulisic and Adrien Rabiot, inflicting Inter’s first Serie A defeat.

The Rossoneri were stung by their first Serie A defeat of the season, losing here 4-2 to Bologna in the midweek round. While Fikayo Tomori joined Matteo Gabbia, Samuel Chukwueze, Ruben Loftus-Cheek, Alexis Saelemaekers and Pietro Terracciano on the absentee list, they did bring back Adrien Rabiot, Mario Gila and Diego Moreira.

The Nerazzurri were top of the table after two lucky late wins over Fiorentina and Venezia, but Lautaro Martinez, Luis Henrique, Curtis Jones, John Stones and Benjamin Pavard were still out, while Ivan Provedel had flu. Cristian Chivu surprised with a 3-4-2-1 formation, using Andy Diouf and Petar Sucic in new roles.

Milan had won the last three competitive meetings with their city rivals without conceding a goal and were unbeaten against them since a 2-1 result in April 2024.

It was also a very special occasion, the first Derby della Madonnina since the Stadio Meazza turned 100, and with plans for the new Generali Stadium unveiled just 48 hours ago.
