Non-US Investing • Re: US Citizen / Retire in Ireland -&gt; Taxes

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →
In looking over what your wrote Ferd, I would say that your opinion is unsubstantiated and misleading.
I am assuming you both are not Irish citizens, is that correct? If you were, you would already be taxed on your global income.

A few other points. If you do not know what domicile means, at least the Irish interpretation, I would strongly urge a full understanding, as it a key element in understanding your tax situation.
Yes, Ireland has a sticky definition of domicile. Many Irish people in the US probably have a domicile in Ireland by Irish definition. So what? Let's do tax analysis here.
From Irish Revenue:
"If you are resident and domiciled in Ireland for tax purposes, you are chargeable to tax in Ireland on your worldwide income. Worldwide income is the total income that you earn anywhere in the world in a tax year."
And?
It has everything to do with it. If you are an Irish citizen, you are likely domiciled there. Therefore, the quoted rule applies.
Did you notice the "and"? I mean really did you consider it? Do you think you understand it? My reading of the Irish Revenue statement is that you must be a tax resident to be subject to tax in Ireland on your worldwide income. Just having Irish citizenship and likely having a domicile in Ireland due to the definition doesn't mean you are a tax resident. See https://www.revenue.ie/en/jobs-and-pens ... dents.aspx

In fact, you can be an Irish Citizen and Domiciled in Ireland and not subject to taxation on global income. Or you might be taxed on global passive income in those same circumstances. It depends entirely on how your tax residency is defined (not domicile, tax residency). You may be categorized as a "ordinarily resident" or a "Non-ordinarily resident" and that will determine your taxation.

So no, I am not cherry picking statements. What you wrote about Irish citizenship making Ireland domicile likely and therfore subjecting you to tax in Ireland on your worldwide income is simply incorrect.
