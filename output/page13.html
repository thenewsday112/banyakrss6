Perseverance was only meant to drive 12 miles on Mars but it just broke Opportunity’s record by steering itself

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

On Oct. 11, 2026, NASA’s Perseverance rover drove 299.6 feet (91.31 meters) over rocky ground west of Jezero Crater on Mars.

That drive took its odometer to 28.10 miles (45.22 kilometers). Opportunity, which explored Mars from 2004 to 2018, had stopped at 28.06 miles (45.16 kilometers), the farthest any vehicle had gone on another world.

Perseverance got there in about five and a half years, while Opportunity needed almost 15. The rover picked its own route for more than 90 percent of the distance, but planners on Earth still set the points it drives between.

Sol 2,006 near Lac de Charmes

The record came on the 2,006th Martian day, or sol, of the mission. A navigation camera photographed the scene that day. It shows pale sand, broken slabs of rock and wheel tracks running toward a rising slope.

The rover was heading for an area the team calls “Idubi.” Its route crossed a region nicknamed “Lac de Charmes,” just off the western rim of the crater.

Animated orbital map of the route Perseverance has driven from its 2021 landing to Oct. 11, 2026, the day it passed Opportunity’s off-world driving record. Video: JPLraw (NASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory)

According to NASA’s mission blog, this may be some of the oldest terrain ever explored in the solar system. Its equivalents on Earth were erased long ago by erosion and plate tectonics. At Idubi, the science team hopes to find traces of ancient hydrothermal systems.

Steven Lee is project manager for the Curiosity and Perseverance rovers at NASA’s Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL) in Southern California.

He noted that the original mission called for no more than 12.4 miles (20 kilometers) of driving, a figure the rover has now passed by a wide margin.

Thinking while driving

Most of the distance came from a self-driving system called AutoNav. While the rover rolls, it photographs the ground ahead and builds a 3D map of hazards. Then it plots a path between waypoints chosen by rover planners on Earth.

The team calls this “thinking while driving.” In an official NASA video from 2022, JPL rover driver Tyler Del Sesto explained that earlier rovers like Curiosity had to stop, take pictures and process them before picking a safe path.

“We give it specific stops along the route, but it chooses how to get there,” he said.

Spirit and Opportunity drove only about 5 percent of their distance with their early autonomous system. Sojourner, NASA’s first Mars rover, judged the path ahead from 20 measurement points.

Perseverance processes 600,000 with every look and has covered up to 1,351 feet (412 meters) in a single sol.

Navigation camera images of Perseverance’s record single-sol drive of 1,350.7 feet (411.7 meters) on June 19, 2025, combined with rover data in a 3D virtual reconstruction. Video: JPLraw (NASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory)

In February 2026, JPL said the rover could now find its own position with Mars Global Localization. The software matches navigation camera panoramas to orbital maps. It takes about two minutes and places the rover within about 10 inches (25 centimeters).

Vandi Verma, chief engineer of robotics operations for the mission, compared it to “giving the rover GPS.” With it, Perseverance learned to find its own way around Mars without waiting a day for mapping experts.

In a December 2025 test, the rover completed drives with waypoints planned by generative AI instead of human operators.

Sojourner, Spirit and Opportunity

Sojourner was about the size of a microwave oven. It covered about 330 feet (100 meters) during its 83-sol mission in 1997.

Distances driven by rovers on the Moon and Mars. Perseverance and Curiosity are still operational, and their figures are as of Oct. 11, 2026. Credit: NASA/JPL-Caltech. Click image to enlarge.

Spirit and Opportunity landed in January 2004, with prime missions planned for 90 sols. Spirit logged 4.8 miles (7.7 kilometers) before it stopped in the Martian winter of 2010. In 2024, the twin rovers marked 20 years since landing.

Opportunity kept rolling for 5,111 sols. According to NASA, it passed a marathon distance of 26.219 miles (42.195 kilometers) in 2015. A planet-wide dust storm covered its site in June 2018. Its last signal reached Earth on June 10.

Curiosity landed in Gale Crater in 2012 and is still working. In its record announcement, NASA put Curiosity’s total at more than 23.65 miles (38.07 kilometers).

Apollo 17 and Lunokhod 2

The record also covers drives on the Moon, where Apollo 17 astronauts Gene Cernan and Harrison Schmitt covered 22.2 miles (35.7 kilometers) in their lunar rover in 1972. The Soviet Union’s robotic Lunokhod 2 drove 24.2 miles (39 kilometers) in 1973.

Perseverance passed both marks earlier in its trip. Opportunity had also beaten them during its own travels.

Mark Maimone is deputy team chief for robotic operations on Perseverance. He said the rover broke the record “by making its own decisions about the path.” He added that “Perseverance is a better driver than the one that landed, and we’re not done.”

Glare still ends afternoon drives

When Perseverance is heading toward the Sun, glare in the navigation cameras forces the team to stop by mid-afternoon, Maimone said.

The team wants to teach the rover to see past that glare, which Maimone said would give every rover that follows a longer working day.

Human planners on Earth still pick the waypoints for routine drives, so AutoNav does not choose where the rover goes on them.

Lee expects the record to keep growing for many years, since all of the rover’s mobility systems still work and extended lifespan tests on the ground have come back favorable.

In December 2025, JPL reported that the rotary actuators turning the wheels were certified for at least another 37 miles (60 kilometers), with nearly all subsystems judged able to work until at least 2031.

Because Perseverance and Curiosity are both still moving, the totals in NASA’s comparison are only a snapshot of Oct. 11.

Toward Idubi and Rainbow Falls

NASA approved the first mission extension, which began in October 2026 and runs to about the end of 2028. Perseverance has collected 29 rock and regolith samples. They come from the crater floor, an old river delta and the crater rim.

On its way up, the rover found a potential biosignature at a rock called Cheyava Falls. On the rim, it has studied rocks made by impacts about 4 billion years ago.

After Lac de Charmes, the rover will follow the rim south toward Rainbow Falls. That block of ancient crust is 328 feet (100 meters) wide and was pushed up by the impact that made Jezero.

The navigation camera photo from Oct. 11 shows wheel tracks pressed into the sand on the day the record fell. Every drive since then has added to a distance no other vehicle has reached on another world.

The information was obtained from NASA Science.

Spurs 121, Wolves 111: Couldn’t Get Over the Hump

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →
MINNEAPOLIS, MINNESOTA - OCTOBER 30: LaMelo Ball #1 of the Minnesota Timberwolves celebrates his three-point basket against the Los Angeles Clippers in the fourth quarter of the NBA Cup game at Target Center on October 30, 2026 in Minneapolis, Minnesota. On the eighth anniversary of Derrick Rose’s 50 point game, your Minnesota Timberwolves fell victim to a Halloween monster performance by alien Victor Wembanyama, losing to the San Antonio Spurs by a score of 121-111. 

With this being a second night of a set of back-to-back games and playing without Anthony Edwards and Rudy Gobert (back spasms), many fans may have expected tonight’s result.

What they may not have expected, however, was the amount of fight the Wolves had in them even if it was not enough for the victory. 

Encouraging Signs from Joan Beringer

A performance I really want to highlight was that of Joan Beringer. Starting tonight in place of Rudy Gobert, Joan poured in 12 points, grabbed six rebounds, and committed five fouls in 34 minutes of play.

On paper, his French counterpart completely eviscerated him, with Wemby scoring an otherworldly 43 points on 17-34 shooting.

Looks like Wemby dominated him, right? It did feel like that at times, because Victor is such a freak. Every time Wemby came at Joan with something ridiculous, Joan just kept battling.

It was amazing growth to see, because at age 34 Rudy Gobert is going to miss games. Joan Beringer needs to be effective in those games, and although the stats didn’t show it tonight, he was able to hold his own. 

Ayo Continues His Steady Play

Ayo Dosunmu continues to fill in admirably in the absence of Anthony Edwards, scoring 27 points on 11-15 shooting, splashing two threes, and even grabbing nine boards.

These types of performances are exactly why he was paid all that money to come back.

His speed in the open floor is outrageous, his willingness to defend, and his overall tenacity is exactly what this team will need come playoff time, but they need it even more now that Ant remains sidelined. 

Tough Night for ‘Melo

Unfortunately, LaMelo Ball had a rough night. He had just 10 points, 2/10 shooting overall, and didn’t make a three. He spent much of the game on the bench due to foul trouble, and even fouled out at the end of the game when the Wolves were attempting to make a final push at victory.

It’s becoming a pattern now where LaMelo puts himself in these disadvantageous situations and it’s not something the Wolves can afford, especially on a back-to-back in San Antonio. He looks sped up, can’t find any rhythm with his shot, and is playing with his hands too much on the defensive end. 

You maybe can get away with a performance like this against the NBA’s cellar dwellers, but not against the reigning Western Conference champions.

Moving forward, I would like to see LaMelo get himself going by attacking the paint and maybe getting to the free throw line or making easy dump offs for his teammates. Finding a rhythm isn’t just about making shots, it’s about finding your place in an offense on game in,, game out basis. The shots will start falling, just not if you force the issue too much. 

Jonathan Kuminga Making Plays

Among the positives, Jonathan Kuminga I thought had a really nice game. He logged 40 minutes, which was good to see after only 22 last night, scoring 19 points, hitting two threes, and really helping defend the powerhouse San Antonio guards on the perimeter. Performances like these are why people keep falling in love with this man’s game. It wasn’t totally flashy, either, save for this dunk on Victor Wembanyama.

For some reason, I feel like we’re on borrowed time with Kuminga. It’s his reputation, based on his last two stops. Chris Finch is really good at defining roles for his players, and if this is the Jonathan Kuminga we’re going to see more often than not then I’m all for it. 

Lackluster Bench Night

Bench wise, there wasn’t really a ton of note that happened tonight. The presumed sixth and seventh men were starting tonight, so Finch only went nine deep.

Outside of some Jaylen Clark made threes, there wasn’t a ton of production. TJ Shannon had a game he’d like to forget, making only one field goal and failing to draw iron on a couple threes. Trey Lyles made some nice plays, but also looked lost at times. 

I was a little shocked that we didn’t see Cody Williams tonight. Without a true center coming off the bench, he’s someone with some size that I thought he could go throw his body around and battle among the tree that is Wemby. 

Another head scratcher was the lack of Bones Hyland out there. With LaMelo down in foul trouble early, he came into the game and got six pretty quick points, but wasn’t really effective in his second stint and we didn’t really see him again. That could be a product of Ayo having a nice night, but it seemed as if he could’ve helped settle the offense down and there were times tonight where that was sorely needed.  

Uneven Officiating

One last quick thing before we flush this one and move on to the next game: The way this team gets officiated is mind-boggling at times. I’m not saying that the majority of the fouls that were called on Minnesota tonight were bad calls, but a lot of the touchy calls that they get called on them don’t seem to go the other way when they happen to the Timberwolves. 

Case in point, there was a sequence when Joan Beringer made an effort play on Victor Wembanyama to get a putback and keep the game tight and got a foul for going over the back of Wembanyama.

It’s totally fine if that’s the level of contact the officials want to whistle for. The ensuing possession down the floor, Wemby gets quite physical with Beringer in order to make himself available down low (a level of physicality you wouldn’t know Wemby had if you watched him drive to the hoop, just saying) and there was nothing called as Wemby almost put Joan in the rim. Add that on to the lack of calls Anthony Edwards gets going to the hole, and it creates a discrepancy that the Wolves should take issue with. 

Final Thoughts

All in all, there were plenty of positives to take away from tonight’s game in San Antonio. The Timberwolves fought valiantly to keep themselves in the game, but just couldn’t quite get over the hump.

Colors of the Moon

Explanation: What color is the Moon? It depends on the night — and the time. Outside of the Earth’s atmosphere, the dark Moon, which shines by reflected sunlight, appears a magnificently brown-tinged gray. The featured image highlights a collection of apparent colors of the full moon on Earth, documented by one astrophotographer over 10 years from different locations across Italy. A red– or yellow-colored moon usually indicates a moon seen near the horizon. There, some of the blue light has been scattered away by a long path through the Earth’s atmosphere, sometimes laden with fine dust. A blue-colored moon is more rare and can indicate an atmosphere carrying larger dust particles. What created the purple moon is unclear — it may be a combination of several effects. The last image captures the total lunar eclipse of 2018 July — where the moon, in Earth’s shadow, appeared a faint red — due to light refracted through air around the Earth. Have you ever seen an unusually colorful Moon?

'90s Grunge Band's Iconic Album That Almost Was a Disaster Hits No. 2 Thirty Years Later

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Thirty years ago, a legendary grunge group released an album that would become a testament to what made them one of the greatest rock bands of all time. Now, Alice in Chains’ Unplugged is suddenly charting. The record’s legendary status and recent surge up the charts are even more remarkable given how close it came to being a massive disaster.

‘Unplugged’ by Alice in Chains Is Charting on Billboard’s Top Album Sales Chart

Recorded on April 10, 1996, Alice in Chains Unplugged is widely regarded as one of the best MTV Unplugged sessions and a masterpiece on its own terms. Featuring Layne Staley and Jerry Cantrell’s incredible harmonies and raw emotion, the album has given listeners chills for more than three decades. In fact, a July 2019 Louder article argued it “is the best live album ever made.”

Now, more than 30 years after its original release on July 30, 1996, Unplugged continues to prove it is an all-time great album. After it was reissued on vinyl on September 25, 2026, to celebrate the record’s 30th anniversary, Unplugged reached No. 2 on Billboard’s Top Album Sales chart for the week ending October 10, 2026. Still going strong today, the record is currently ranked at No. 36 on the chart.

  • 13. “The Killer Is Me”

Why ‘Unplugged’ by Alice in Chains Was Nearly a Disaster

In one key way, Alice in Chains was perfectly positioned to make its Unplugged album a masterpiece. While the band is remembered for its heavy sound and electric guitar solos, it released two acoustic EPs, 1992’s Sap and 1994’s Jar of Flies. Despite that, circumstances existed that should have doomed Alice in Chains Unplugged.

During his life, Layne Staley proved himself to be a true artist and powerful vocalist whose performances continue to stun people to this day. As such, anyone who whittles down his legacy and makes it about his substance use disorder is doing a massive disservice to him, Alice in Chains, and themselves. Still, that doesn’t mean the effects of his struggles should be ignored, especially when considering why Alice in Chains Unplugged almost became a disaster.

As a Grunge Magazine article updated in May 2025 noted, Staley was suffering from severe withdrawal on the day of the recording of Unplugged. As a result of the physical toll it took on him, he struggled to execute some lyrics and required multiple takes of several tracks. Even though songs took several attempts, the magic that makes him one of the best rock vocalists of all time was still captured in the end.

Additionally, Alice in Chains’ other vocalist was horribly sick during the recording. As Jerry Cantrell explained during a February 2025 Broken Record podcast appearance, which Ultimate Guitar summarized, he was so ill that he needed a garbage can on stage.

“I was sick as a dog. I was not feeling well. And if you look very closely, there’s a trash can at my feet for me to vomit in… When the light went green, and the cameras went on, and we started playing, I got a little adrenaline rush,” Cantrell remembered. “So that turned out to be one of the greatest shows we’ve ever played. I was feeling like f*****g s**t until it started, and then as soon as the show was over, I went back to feeling like s**t and back on the IV.”

If all that wasn’t enough, it had been more than two and a half years since Alice in Chains had performed a full-length public concert together when Unplugged was recorded. Yet, the band overcame it all to create a masterpiece so timeless that it has returned to the charts three decades later and will surely be celebrated for years to come.

Aave Expands Stock-Backed USDC Loans on Base for Eligible Investors

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

TL;DR: The case study listed seven stocks accepted as collateral at the time and said the market supports continuous borrowing and repayment. Geographic eligibility, securities rights, market liquidity and oracle design matter.

A Base case study dated October 22 described Aave V4’s Equities Hub, launched in September, allowing eligible non-U.S. investors to borrow USDC against selected Coinbase tokenized U.S. equities without first selling the shares.

The announcement and the numbers

The most concrete starting point is the dated disclosure from Aave. It offers a specific basis for assessing Aave’s tokenized-equity lending on Base without turning the story into unsupported price speculation.

The case study listed seven stocks accepted as collateral at the time and said the market supports continuous borrowing and repayment. Collateral valuations rely on price feeds whose equity-market coverage differs across weekday and off-hours trading.

There is a practical market question underneath the announcement: how quickly can USDC borrowing against tokenized equities change real activity, rather than merely expand a list of proposed services? For investors, the distinction matters because product news and actual changes to liquidity or demand are not interchangeable.

Why the development matters

The question for market participants is what Aave’s tokenized-equity lending on Base establishes today and what remains uncertain. An immediate reaction is not the same as evidence of a durable economic outcome.

The application demonstrates how programmable securities could gain uses beyond simple transfers. Combining equity exposure with on-chain lending, however, also connects traditional market schedules and corporate events to DeFi risk engines.

DeFi applications make financial rules executable through code, but code still depends on market inputs, transaction ordering and the availability of liquidity. A smart contract can follow its programmed conditions precisely even when those conditions produce an outcome a user did not expect.

From a market perspective, the first task is to separate the scale of the announcement from the scale of economic activity that has actually occurred. A company may report a large addressable market, an institutional name or a future capability, but those are not equivalent to settled transactions or recurring fee income. This is why the primary disclosure is a better starting point than a trading narrative attached to it.

Risks the headline does not resolve

None of this removes the need for independent assessment. The published evidence identifies a development, while its risk and long-term economics are still open to examination.

Borrowing against a tokenized stock lets an investor obtain liquidity while retaining market exposure to the share. That introduces leverage: if the value of collateral falls relative to the debt, a protocol may require repayment or liquidate some of the collateral.

Geographic eligibility, securities rights, market liquidity and oracle design matter. A continuously open loan market does not mean the underlying stock’s cash market is open or that token redemptions are instantaneous under every condition.

For developers, execution quality involves more than a quoted price or an advertised feature. Gas costs, settlement success, oracle quality, permissions and incident handling all shape whether an integration is safe and convenient in real markets.

The milestones to watch

The immediate checklist is relatively straightforward: look for verifiable implementation details and avoid interpreting a stated target as an outcome that has already occurred.

Watch whether additional equity collateral is approved and how the lending market handles holidays, sudden gaps in stock prices and unexpected liquidations. A useful product must keep those risks understandable to borrowers.

Investors also need to separate changes in infrastructure from changes in token demand. Revenue may accrue to a company, a protocol treasury or a service provider without necessarily creating a one-for-one benefit for holders of a related token. Market impact depends on adoption, competition and the design of whatever fees or incentives are involved.

A durable signal would be consistent follow-through: actual usage, transparent reporting and a credible explanation of risk. Short-term token-price moves can be driven by macroeconomic conditions or leverage and may say little about whether the product or initiative succeeds over months and years.
